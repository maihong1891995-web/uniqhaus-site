Almost every real bathroom remodel in California needs a building permit - and in San Jose that means a fee of roughly $400-$1,500, filed online at SJPermits.org and issued in about 1-3 weeks. The line most homeowners miss is where the exemption sits: a permit is triggered the moment you move plumbing or electrical, change the layout, or alter walls, while a genuine like-for-like swap of a vanity, toilet, or tile in the same spots often needs no permit at all. Do you need a permit to remodel a bathroom in California?

Yes - you need a permit for any California bathroom remodel that moves plumbing or electrical, changes the layout, or alters walls, and in San Jose that permit costs $400-$1,500. California permitting is administered locally, so the exact fee and process run through your city or county building department, but the trigger is the same statewide: the moment work touches structure, plumbing, or wiring, a permit is required. Because most mid-range and master bathroom remodels involve new shower valves, added circuits, exhaust fans, or relocated fixtures, the honest answer for the majority of projects is that a permit is needed.

The one common exception is a true cosmetic refresh. If you replace a vanity, toilet, and tile in the exact same positions, with no new wiring, no re-routed plumbing, and no wall changes, that like-for-like swap is often exempt. The trouble is that homeowners tend to underestimate their own scope - adding a recessed light or moving a shower head six inches quietly puts the project back into permit territory. When in doubt, a quick call to the building department or a permitting service settles it before demolition, not after.

What work requires a permit vs what is exempt

As a rule, structural, plumbing, and electrical changes require a permit, while surface-level replacements in the same footprint do not - and the single dividing line is whether anything moves. The table below maps the most common San Jose bathroom scopes to each side of that line.

The moment you decide to move the shower or open a wall, budget for both the extra $3,000-$8,000 in construction and the permit that comes with it. How long does a bathroom permit take?

A San Jose bathroom permit is typically issued in about 1-3 weeks, with another 2-4 weeks added if your project requires plan review. Within the overall 8-14 week bathroom remodel timeline, permitting occupies roughly the same 2-4 week window - and because it can overlap with design and material ordering when one team runs the project, it usually does not extend the calendar the way homeowners fear.

Simpler scopes - a new shower valve, an added circuit, a fan - often clear over the counter or with a fast online issuance. A remodel that moves walls or significantly re-plumbs the room is the kind that gets routed to plan review, which is where the extra 2-4 weeks comes from. The way to protect the schedule is to submit complete, code-current drawings the first time; incomplete submittals are the most common cause of a permit dragging. What happens if you remodel without a permit?

Skipping a required permit can cost far more than the $400-$1,500 you avoided - unpermitted work often has to be opened up, inspected, and sometimes redone to meet code, and it must be disclosed when you sell. In a Santa Clara County market where an outdated or non-compliant bathroom already suppresses offers, unpermitted work is a red flag that can lower a buyer's price or stall escrow while it gets legalized after the fact. The savings are almost always illusory.

There is a code dimension too. The 2025 California Building Codes, in effect since January 1, 2026, set water-efficiency standards for toilets, showerheads, and faucets, and a permitted project is checked against them at review and inspection. Work done without a permit skips that check, so it can quietly fall out of compliance - which becomes your problem at resale or when an insurance claim turns up the change. Pulling the permit is the cheap insurance: it documents that the plumbing, electrical, and waterproofing were done to standard.
