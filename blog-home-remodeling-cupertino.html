Cupertino is one of the Bay Area's most sought-after places to own a home, and most of its houses were built decades ago. That combination - high demand and aging homes - makes remodeling the default path for families who want a modern home without leaving the neighborhood. This guide covers what a Cupertino home remodel costs, how permits work through the City of Cupertino, the housing stock you are likely working with, and what to plan for before you start. It pairs with our Cupertino service area page and the broader whole house remodel cost guide.

How much does a home remodel cost in Cupertino?

A home remodel in Cupertino typically costs $200 to $450+ per square foot, in line with Bay Area whole-home remodel pricing of roughly $100 to $500 per square foot depending on scope and finish level. Cosmetic refreshes sit at the low end; a down-to-studs gut with system upgrades sits at the top. Older 1960s and 1970s homes often need electrical, plumbing, and structural work that pushes the number higher.

Permits in Cupertino

Almost every Cupertino remodel beyond simple cosmetic work needs a permit, and permits are issued by the City of Cupertino Community Development Department and its Building Division. You will typically pull a mix of the following, depending on scope:

  • Building permit for structural work, additions, and layout changes.
  • Electrical permit for rewiring, new circuits, and panel upgrades - common in older homes.
  • Plumbing permit for repiping, moving fixtures, and new bathrooms or kitchens.
  • Mechanical permit for HVAC changes and new heating or cooling systems.

Additions and second-story projects also trigger planning review before the building permit is issued, because they affect lot coverage, setbacks, and height. Planning review is the step that most often stretches the schedule, so it should be started early. We handle submittal and plan check directly with the City of Cupertino as part of our architecture and permitting service, so the design, permit path, and build stay on one track.

Cupertino housing stock

Most of Cupertino's single-family homes are 1960s and 1970s ranch and tract houses, built during the valley's postwar expansion. They are typically single-story, on flat lots, with compartmentalized floor plans, original or dated systems, and lower ceilings than today's homes. That makes them excellent remodel candidates: sound bones, good lots, and clear opportunities to modernize.

The most common moves on these homes are opening up the kitchen and living areas, updating aging electrical and plumbing, improving insulation and windows, and expanding the footprint or adding a second story to gain space on a fixed lot. Homes of this era often hide knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring, galvanized plumbing, and undersized panels, so budgeting for system upgrades is realistic. Our guide to updating a 1970s or 1980s home goes deeper on what to expect behind the walls.

What to plan for in Cupertino

Beyond cost and permits, a few things shape Cupertino remodels more than in neighboring cities:

  • School-driven demand. Cupertino's top-rated schools keep families rooted in place, so owners invest heavily to stay rather than move. That pushes many toward whole-house remodels and additions rather than quick cosmetic updates.
  • Older systems. With most homes 50-plus years old, expect to update wiring, plumbing, HVAC, and insulation. Plan the budget and timeline around system work, not just finishes.
  • Additions and second stories. Because lots are fixed and homes are often single-story, going up or out is a frequent way to gain space - and both need planning review plus engineering.
  • Lot coverage and setback rules. The City of Cupertino limits how much of a lot can be built on and how close to property lines you can build. These rules decide what an addition or second story can actually look like, so they are worth checking before you fall in love with a plan.

Getting these right early is what keeps a Cupertino project on budget, because the expensive surprises here are usually behind the walls or inside the zoning code, not in the finishes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a home remodel cost in Cupertino?

A home remodel in Cupertino typically costs $200 to $450+ per square foot. Cosmetic updates run about $100 to $200 per square foot, a mid-range whole-house remodel about $200 to $350, and a high-end or down-to-studs gut $350 to $500+. Older 1960s and 1970s homes often need system upgrades that push costs toward the higher end.

Do I need a permit to remodel in Cupertino?

Yes. Almost every Cupertino remodel beyond simple cosmetic work needs a permit through the City of Cupertino Community Development Department and Building Division. Building, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical permits cover structural, wiring, plumbing, and HVAC changes, and additions or second stories also require planning review.

Can I add a second story in Cupertino?

Often yes. Many Cupertino homes are single-story 1960s and 1970s ranch and tract houses, and owners frequently add a second story to gain space on a fixed lot. A second story requires planning review plus a building permit, and it must meet the city's lot coverage, setback, and height rules.

Why do people remodel in Cupertino?

The biggest driver is Cupertino's top-rated schools, which keep demand high and make owners invest in staying rather than moving. Families remodel and expand older single-story homes to fit modern life while keeping their school assignment, so whole-house updates and additions are especially common here.

How long does a Cupertino remodel take?

Plan on about 2 to 3 months for design, engineering, and permitting through the City of Cupertino, then roughly 4 to 9 months of construction for a whole-house remodel. A second-story addition or full gut runs longer. Planning review for additions is the variable that moves the schedule most.
