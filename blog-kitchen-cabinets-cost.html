If one line item decides your kitchen budget, it is the cabinets. In a Bay Area remodel they are the single largest cost at 29-40% of the total - about $18,000-$34,000 on a mid-range project - which means the choice between stock, semi-custom, and full custom moves your number more than the countertops, appliances, and flooring combined. This guide breaks down what cabinets actually cost per linear foot, why they dominate the budget, which materials and door styles are worth it, and where to save without ending up with drawers that sag in five years. It is a companion to our full San Jose kitchen remodel guide; if your space is compact, pair it with our small kitchen remodel ideas and cost guide.

How much do kitchen cabinets cost in the Bay Area?

Kitchen cabinets cost roughly $18,000 to $34,000 on a mid-range Bay Area kitchen in 2026, which is 29-40% of the whole remodel budget. Cabinets are sold by the linear foot of run - the length of counter and wall they occupy - and a typical kitchen carries 25 to 30 linear feet across base cabinets, wall cabinets, and an island. Multiply your linear footage by the per-foot rate for your grade and you have a fast, realistic cabinet estimate before you ever meet a contractor.

The reason the range is so wide is that "cabinets" spans three very different products. Stock cabinets come flat-packed or pre-built in fixed sizes; semi-custom start from those boxes but let you change dimensions, finishes, and doors; full custom are built from scratch to your exact kitchen. Within a mid-range San Jose kitchen (which runs $55K-$85K in total), cabinets are almost always the biggest single check you write, so it pays to understand the grades before you spend.

Cabinet grade and cost per linear foot

The three cabinet grades break down into clear 2026 Bay Area price bands per linear foot, and the gap between them is large: full custom can cost four to ten times what stock does for the same run. Matching the grade to how long you will keep the kitchen - and how unusual its layout is - is the fastest way to a budget that fits.

GradeCost per linear footWhat you get
Stock$100-$300Fixed sizes off the shelf, limited finishes, fast lead time - fillers cover any gaps
Semi-custom$150-$700Stock boxes with custom sizes, finishes, and door styles - the Bay Area sweet spot
Full custom$500-$1,200+Built to exact dimensions and materials, any style, uses every inch of an odd layout

For most homeowners keeping the existing footprint, semi-custom hits the value target: you get the sizing and finish flexibility that makes a kitchen feel bespoke without paying custom-shop labor. Full custom earns its premium in two cases - a genuinely irregular layout where stock boxes waste space, or a design where a specific wood, slab door, or seamless run is the whole point. Stock is the right call for a rental, a flip, or a tight budget where speed matters. Our kitchen remodel guide shows how each grade fits the larger cost tiers.

Why cabinets are the biggest line in a kitchen

Cabinets top the budget because they combine three expensive things in one product - a lot of material, precision installation, and hardware - stretched across the longest run in the room. Where a countertop is a single surface and a faucet is one fixture, cabinets are 25 to 30 linear feet of built millwork that has to be level, plumb, and scribed to walls that are rarely square. Because cabinets are such a large slice, they are also the biggest lever on your total - a good reason to get the grade decision right before shopping for the fun finishes. It cuts both ways: trading full custom for semi-custom can save $10,000 or more, while jumping from stock to custom quietly doubles the largest line on the estimate. Cabinet materials and door styles

Two things drive both the price and the lifespan of a cabinet: the box material and the door. The box is what everything hangs on, so it matters more than most homeowners realize. In order of durability and cost, the common choices are:

  • Plywood boxes: the strongest mainstream option - it holds screws, resists Bay Area humidity, and carries the weight of stone counters without sagging. Worth the upgrade over particleboard on almost any project you plan to keep.
  • MDF: smooth, stable, and excellent for painted doors because it will not show wood grain or telegraph joints - but heavier and less water-tolerant than plywood if a leak goes unnoticed.
  • Particleboard / melamine: the cheapest box, standard in entry-level stock lines - fine for a rental or flip, but it swells if it gets wet and strips out at the screws over time.

Door style is where cabinets show their price and set the whole look of the kitchen. A Shaker door - a simple five-piece frame - is the Bay Area default because it reads clean and timeless and costs less than ornate options. Flat-slab doors suit a modern, minimalist kitchen and are easy to clean; inset doors that sit flush inside the frame are the most expensive because they demand precise, custom-grade fitting. A painted finish costs more than stained wood, and a specialty color or thermofoil wrap adds again. The move that protects all of it is quality hardware - soft-close hinges and full-extension drawer glides are what make a cabinet feel expensive every day, so they are the last place to economize. How to save on cabinets without cutting quality

The single biggest saving on cabinets is to keep the existing kitchen layout so your cabinet runs stay the same length and nothing has to be reconfigured or added. Moving the sink or range lengthens runs, adds fillers, and often pushes you toward custom sizing - so a like-for-like footprint keeps you in the affordable end of your chosen grade. From there, a handful of moves trim the largest line without making the kitchen feel cheap:

  • Choose semi-custom over full custom. You keep the sizing and finish flexibility that makes a kitchen feel tailored while avoiding custom-shop labor - often a $10,000-plus difference on a mid-range kitchen.
  • Reface or reuse solid boxes. If the existing carcasses are plywood and square, new doors, drawer fronts, and hardware can transform the look for a fraction of full replacement.
  • Spend on what you touch, save on what you don't. Put the money into doors, soft-close hinges, and drawer glides; use plain interiors, standard shelving, and fillers where no one looks.
  • Pick a Shaker or flat-slab door. Simple door styles cost less than inset or ornate profiles and stay in style far longer, protecting resale.

What you should not cut is the box material or the hardware - a particleboard carcass with builder-grade hinges is exactly the cabinet that fails first, and replacing it later costs far more than doing it right once. A minor cosmetic kitchen refresh recoups about 113% of its cost nationally (roughly 122% in California) in 2025 Cost vs. Value data, so a smart cabinet spend is one of the more reliable resale investments in the house. Frequently Asked Questions

How much do kitchen cabinets cost in the Bay Area?

What is the cost per linear foot for kitchen cabinets?

Kitchen cabinets are priced per linear foot of run. In 2026 the Bay Area ranges are stock $100-$300, semi-custom $150-$700, and full custom $500-$1,200 or more per linear foot. A typical kitchen has 25-30 linear feet of cabinetry across base and wall runs.

What percentage of a kitchen remodel is cabinets?

Cabinets are the single largest line in a kitchen remodel at 29-40% of the budget - about $18,000-$34,000 on a mid-range Bay Area kitchen. They carry material, hardware, and skilled installation across a large run, so no other category comes close.

What is the difference between stock, semi-custom, and full custom cabinets?

Stock cabinets come in fixed sizes off the shelf and are the most affordable. Semi-custom start from stock boxes but allow size, finish, and door changes. Full custom are built to your exact dimensions and materials, cost the most, and use every inch of an odd layout.

What cabinet material is best for the money?

Plywood boxes with solid-wood or MDF Shaker doors give the best value in the Bay Area - stronger and more moisture-resistant than particleboard, at far less than full custom. Match a durable box to a simple door style and spend the savings on quality hinges and drawer glides.

How can I save on kitchen cabinets without cutting quality?

Keep the existing kitchen layout so cabinet runs stay the same, choose semi-custom over full custom, and reface or reuse boxes that are still solid. Spend on the doors, hardware, and soft-close hinges you touch daily, and save on interiors and fillers you never see.
