A whole-house remodel is the largest home project most families ever take on, and in San Jose it carries the full Silicon Valley premium. In 2026, expect anywhere from $150,000 for a cosmetic refresh of a typical home to $600,000 or more for a mid-to-high-end renovation, with a down-to-studs luxury gut able to exceed $1,000,000. How much does a whole-house remodel cost in San Jose in 2026?

A whole-house remodel in San Jose costs $150,000 to $600,000 or more in 2026 for a typical 1,500-2,500 square foot home, or roughly $100 to $500+ per square foot depending on how deep the work goes. Those Bay Area rates run about 20-30% above the national average because of Silicon Valley labor and material costs. The range is enormous because "whole-house remodel" spans everything from new finishes throughout to taking a home down to its studs and rebuilding every system.

Your final number is set by two things: the level of the remodel (cosmetic, mid-range, or full gut) and the size of the home. A cosmetic refresh keeps the layout and systems and updates surfaces; a mid-range remodel reworks kitchens, baths, and some walls; a full gut rebuilds structure, wiring, plumbing, and layout from scratch. The tables below map both levels and home sizes to real 2026 pricing, and the same logic runs through our whole house remodel service in San Jose.

Whole-house remodel cost breakdown

On a whole-house project, the kitchen and bathrooms dominate the budget: a mid-range kitchen runs about $55K-$85K and a mid-range bathroom about $30K-$55K in San Jose, so two baths and a kitchen alone can be half the total. Understanding this split tells you where design choices move the number and where they barely do. The table below shows a typical allocation for a mid-range whole-house remodel.

Two things stand out. First, the wet rooms carry the budget, which is why our kitchen remodeling and bathroom remodeling services drive most of the cost planning on a whole-house job. Second, the contingency is not optional - a 10-20% reserve is what keeps a project solvent when walls open up, and design and architecture at 8-15% is money that prevents far more expensive mistakes later.

What drives whole-house remodel cost up the most?

Beyond the kitchen and baths, the single biggest cost surprise on San Jose whole-house remodels is the age of the home. Many Bay Area houses predate 1980, and once walls come off, older systems that must be replaced to pass code appear:

  • Knob-and-tube wiring: obsolete and unsafe by modern standards - a whole-house rewire is a major line item.
  • Galvanized or old plumbing: corroded supply lines that need full replacement before new finishes go in.
  • Foundation issues: settling, cracks, or seismic weaknesses that must be addressed before cosmetic work.
  • Asbestos or lead paint: common in pre-1980 homes and requiring specialized, permitted abatement.

This is why an inspection first is non-negotiable and why you sequence structure and systems before cosmetics - repainting over failing wiring is money thrown away. The other big driver is finish grade: stepping from a cosmetic refresh to a full gut roughly triples the per-square-foot rate, because it adds structural work, new systems, and premium materials all at once. Permits add cost and calendar too - a whole-house remodel almost always requires them for structural, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, or layout changes, filed at SJPermits.org, and our permitting service folds that into the plan.

How to control a whole-house remodel budget

The highest-impact way to control a whole-house budget is to hold a 10-20% contingency and choose a refresh over a full gut wherever the layout already works. A whole-home remodel recoups only about 50-70% overall on resale, and a well-designed refresh beats a full gut on that math - so unless you are building for long-term enjoyment, over-building rarely pays back. Targeted kitchen and bath updates return the most, which is where to concentrate spend.

From there, plan the money and the sequence:

  • Inspect before you budget. Knowing the true condition of wiring, plumbing, and foundation up front prevents the mid-project surprises that blow past a contingency.
  • Spend on the wet rooms, save on the rest. A mid-range kitchen and updated baths capture most of the buyer appeal; hold the line on secondary spaces.
  • Plan financing early. California homeowners commonly use a home-equity loan or HELOC (popular given Bay Area equity), a cash-out refinance, renovation loans such as FHA 203(k) or Fannie Mae HomeStyle, construction-to-permanent loans, contractor or manufacturer financing, or a personal loan. Compare the rate, term, and whether funds release in draws.
  • Budget for a 4-9 month timeline. Design, permitting, and construction on a whole-house remodel run about 4-9 months (a full gut longer), and rushing a compressed schedule usually costs more, not less.

Running design, permitting, and construction under one roof is itself a budget control, because a single team pricing and building the work removes the handoffs where scope and cost tend to drift.
