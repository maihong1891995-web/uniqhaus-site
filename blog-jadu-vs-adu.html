JADU vs ADU: What Is the Difference? (California 2026)

If you are researching a backyard rental unit in the Bay Area, you have run into two acronyms that sound the same but follow very different rules: the JADU (junior accessory dwelling unit) and the ADU (accessory dwelling unit). The short version is that a JADU is a small unit carved out of your existing home - up to 500 square feet - while a standard ADU is a larger, fully independent unit of up to 1,200 square feet. That single distinction drives everything else: cost, owner-occupancy, kitchens, bathrooms, and rental income. This spoke extends our complete Bay Area ADU guide and pairs with our ADU floor plans and sizes breakdown, because size is exactly what decides the JADU-versus-ADU question.

JADU vs ADU: what is the difference?

The core difference is that a JADU is created within the existing walls of your home and capped at 500 square feet, whereas a standard ADU is an independent living space that can be attached, detached, or a garage conversion and reaches up to 1,200 square feet. A JADU is typically a converted bedroom or an attached garage bay; it needs its own exterior entrance and a small efficiency kitchen, but California lets it share a bathroom with the main house. A standard ADU is a complete, self-contained home with its own kitchen, full bathroom, and separate utilities.

The other defining split is who has to live on the property. A JADU requires owner-occupancy - you must live in either the JADU or the main house. A standard ADU carries no owner-occupancy requirement, so you can rent out both the main house and the ADU while living somewhere else entirely. That freedom, plus the extra square footage, is why standard ADUs command higher rent, and why the choice is rarely just about money. California rules for each (AB 1154, AB 976, SB 13)

Three state laws set the rules that separate a JADU from a standard ADU, and each one directly affects your budget or your obligations. Knowing which law applies to which unit is what keeps a Bay Area project from stalling at the permit counter.

  • AB 1154 - owner-occupancy for JADUs. A JADU requires the owner to live on the property, in either the JADU or the main house. This is the single biggest strings-attached difference: a JADU is designed for a homeowner who wants a rental or family unit while staying put, not an absentee investor.
  • AB 976 - no owner-occupancy for standard ADUs. This law removed the owner-occupancy requirement for standard ADUs, so you can rent out both the main house and the ADU and live elsewhere. It is the legal reason a standard ADU is the more flexible income property.
  • SB 13 - impact fees waived under 750 sq ft. Any ADU or JADU under 750 square feet pays no impact fees. Because a JADU is capped at 500 square feet, it always qualifies; a compact standard ADU under 750 square feet does too, which can save thousands.

One more rule shapes the bigger picture: AB 1033 now lets some California cities allow an ADU to be sold separately, condo-style, from the main house. That option applies to standard ADUs, not JADUs, and it is another reason a standalone ADU can be the stronger long-term asset. State ADU size floors also matter here - cities must allow at least 850 square feet for a one-bedroom and 1,000 square feet for a two-bedroom ADU. Cost difference between a JADU and an ADU

A JADU costs dramatically less - roughly $50,000 to $150,000 - because it reuses existing conditioned space inside your home, while a standard ADU in San Jose runs $150,000 to $450,000, or about $300 to $500 per square foot. The JADU saves money in three ways: no new foundation or shell, a small efficiency kitchen instead of a full one, and the option to share a bathroom with the house rather than plumb a new one. A standard ADU pays for all of that from scratch, which is why detached units sit at the top of the range and garage conversions or attached units sit lower.

Rent is the other side of the ledger. A JADU studio typically earns about $2,000-$2,500 a month in the 2026 Bay Area market, a one-bedroom ADU around $2,500-$3,000, and a two-bedroom detached unit $3,000-$3,500 or more - rent depends on size, location, and finish. Because impact fees are waived on both units under 750 square feet (SB 13), a JADU's lower build cost paired with solid studio rent often makes it the fastest payback, even though a standard ADU earns more per month. Which one should you build?

Build a JADU if you have spare space inside the home - an unused bedroom or an attached garage - want the lowest possible cost, and are comfortable with the owner-occupancy requirement. It is the right call for a homeowner who wants rental income or a unit for family (an aging parent or an adult child) while continuing to live on the property. The build is faster and less disruptive because you are working within the existing footprint rather than pouring a new foundation.

Build a standard ADU if you want more living space, the highest rent, and the freedom to rent the whole property without living there. A detached ADU is the strongest choice for long-term investment: it adds the most square footage, earns $3,000-$3,500+ a month, and under AB 1033 may one day be sold separately in some cities. And you are not strictly choosing one - California lets a single-family lot add one JADU inside the home plus one detached ADU, so owners with the budget and the yard often build both. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a JADU and an ADU?

A JADU (junior ADU) is up to 500 square feet, created within the existing walls of your home, may share a bathroom with the main house, and requires the owner to live on the property. A standard ADU is a fully independent unit up to 1,200 square feet with no owner-occupancy requirement.

Does a JADU require owner-occupancy?

Yes. Under AB 1154, a JADU requires the owner to live in either the JADU or the main house. AB 976 removed the owner-occupancy requirement for standard ADUs, so you can rent out both the house and a standard ADU while living elsewhere.

How much cheaper is a JADU than an ADU?

A JADU typically costs $50,000 to $150,000 because it reuses existing conditioned space inside the home. A standard ADU in San Jose runs roughly $150,000 to $450,000, or about $300 to $500 per square foot, with detached units at the higher end.

Can you have both a JADU and an ADU on the same property?

You can have both. California lets a single-family lot add one JADU inside the home plus one detached ADU of up to 1,200 square feet, so many Bay Area owners build both to maximize rental income and flexibility.

Do you pay impact fees on a JADU?

SB 13 waives impact fees on any ADU or JADU under 750 square feet, so a JADU carries no impact fees. A JADU may also share a bathroom with the main house, which cuts plumbing cost, but it must have its own separate entrance and a small kitchen.

Should I build a JADU or an ADU?

Build a JADU if you have spare bedrooms or an attached garage, want the lowest cost, and are comfortable living on the property. Build a standard ADU if you want more space, higher rent ($2,000-$3,500+ a month), and the freedom to rent the whole property without living there.
