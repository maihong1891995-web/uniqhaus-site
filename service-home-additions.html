Addition types

Five ways to add space to a Bay Area home

The right addition depends on what your family is missing, how much yard you have, and what your lot allows. These are the five we build most often.

  • Room addition. A new bedroom, family room, or office of 200-400 sq ft on its own foundation, usually off the back or side of the house. Room addition cost in San Jose
  • Second-story addition. A whole new floor that keeps your yard intact. It needs the existing foundation and framing checked and often reinforced first. Second-story cost and feasibility
  • Primary suite addition. A bedroom, ensuite bathroom, and walk-in closet planned as one private wing, so the parents finally get a door that closes. Primary suite addition guide
  • Bump-out. One wall pushed out a few feet for a larger kitchen, nook, or bathroom, often without a full new foundation. Bump-out and addition cost by type
  • Addition with an attached garage. New living space and a garage designed together, so the roofline, driveway, and entry read as one house. See the Bird Ave addition with attached garage

How an addition comes together

Five steps, in this order.

Lot and zoning check. We confirm setbacks, lot coverage, and height limits so you know what your lot can hold before you pay for design.

Cost overview

What a home addition costs in the Bay Area.

Expect about $400 to $700+ per finished square foot in 2026. Most of our addition projects land between $100,000 and $500,000+.

Bay Area pricing runs roughly 20-30% above the national average. An addition costs more per foot than a remodel because none of the structure exists yet: new footings, framing, roof, and systems all go into the number. Permits are their own line, often $3,000 to $10,000+ in Santa Clara County for plan check, the building permit, school fees, and any impact fees.

The biggest swings come from three choices: building up instead of out, how much plumbing you add (a bathroom or kitchen costs more than a bedroom of the same size), and finish level. We recommend carrying a 10-20% contingency until the design is final. For the line-by-line breakdown, read our home addition cost guide for the Bay Area.

Questions

Home Addition FAQ

How much does a home addition cost per square foot in San Jose?

About $400 to $700+ per finished square foot in 2026, roughly 20-30% above the national average. A 200-400 sq ft room addition typically runs $100,000 to $250,000, and a second story runs $200,000 to $500,000+. Kitchens and bathrooms sit at the high end because of plumbing and waterproofing. See our room addition cost guide for San Jose.

How long does a home addition take?

Most additions take 4 to 8 months: 1 to 2 months of design, 2 to 4 months of permitting and plan review, and 3 to 5 months of construction. An addition paired with a whole-house remodel usually runs longer. Starting with a complete, code-ready permit set is the biggest lever on the schedule.

Do I need a permit for a home addition in San Jose?

Yes, always, because you are adding square footage. In San Jose the application is filed through SJPermits.org, and plan check, the building permit, school fees, and possible impact fees often total $3,000 to $10,000+. Plan review commonly takes 2 to 4 months. We prepare and submit the set and answer plan check comments for you. Details are in our home addition permit guide for Santa Clara County.

Is a second story addition better than a ground-floor addition?

It depends on your lot. A second story keeps your yard and works when setbacks or lot coverage leave no room to build out, but it costs more because the existing structure usually needs reinforcement and the roof comes off for a time. A ground-floor addition costs less and keeps single-level living, but it uses yard space. Our second-story addition guide compares both.

Can we live at home during a home addition?

Often yes for a ground-floor addition, since much of the work happens outside the existing walls until the tie-in. A second-story addition often means moving out for part of construction while the roof is open. We tell you which applies during design, so you can plan housing with real dates. See when to move out during a remodel.

Should I build a home addition or an ADU?

Build an addition if your family needs more room inside the main house, such as bedrooms or a primary suite that shares the kitchen and living areas. Build an ADU if you want a separate unit with its own entry and kitchen for parents, adult children, or rental income; ADUs typically run $150,000 to $400,000. Compare both in our ADU vs home addition guide, or see our ADU service.
