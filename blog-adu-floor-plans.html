Before you price a build or talk setbacks, one decision shapes everything else: the ADU floor plan. Size, layout, and unit type set your budget, your rental income, and even which California laws apply to your project. In 2026, the plans Bay Area homeowners actually build cluster into three sizes - a studio around 350-500 sq ft, a 1-bedroom around 500-750 sq ft, and a 2-bedroom of 800-1,200 sq ft - plus the compact JADU capped at 500 sq ft. What are the most common ADU floor plans and sizes?

The three most common ADU floor plans in California are the studio (about 350-500 sq ft), the 1-bedroom (about 500-750 sq ft), and the 2-bedroom (about 800-1,200 sq ft). A fourth option, the JADU (junior ADU), is capped at 500 sq ft and carved out of the existing home rather than built new. Which one you choose depends less on taste than on your lot size, your budget, and whether the goal is rental income, family space, or a home office.

Layout follows square footage in a fairly predictable way. Under about 500 sq ft you get an open studio - one room that combines living, sleeping, and kitchen, with a separate bathroom. Around 500-750 sq ft you can wall off a true bedroom and add a proper living area. Above 800 sq ft a second bedroom becomes practical, along with in-unit laundry and a larger kitchen. Because detached units in California are allowed up to 1,200 sq ft, that number is the practical ceiling for a single-story backyard ADU. For how these plans translate into build cost, our Bay Area ADU guide covers pricing and permitting end to end.

ADU sizes and layouts by type

Each ADU type maps to a size band and a layout that works within it. The table below shows the four plan types Bay Area homeowners build most often, the typical square footage, and what fits inside each footprint. Use it to match a plan to your goal before you commit to a design.

Plan typeTypical sizeLayout & best use
JADU (junior ADU)Up to 500 sq ftBuilt inside the existing home; efficiency kitchen, may share a bathroom with the main house - lowest-cost path
Studio ADU350-500 sq ftOne open room (living, sleeping, kitchen) plus a full bathroom - single tenant, guest suite, or office
1-bedroom ADU500-750 sq ftSeparate bedroom, living area, full kitchen and bath - the most versatile rental layout
2-bedroom ADU800-1,200 sq ftTwo bedrooms, full kitchen, in-unit laundry, often 1.5-2 baths - family living or top rent

Notice that the bands overlap: a 500 sq ft plan could be a generous studio or a tight 1-bedroom, and 800 sq ft could be a spacious 1-bedroom or a compact 2-bedroom. The right call depends on whether an extra wall (and the bedroom it creates) is worth trading against open living space. A design-build team can test both layouts on your actual lot before you decide.

How California size rules shape your floor plan

State law sets hard boundaries that shape every ADU floor plan in California. A detached ADU is allowed up to 1,200 sq ft, and a JADU is capped at 500 sq ft. Just as important, cities must allow at least 850 sq ft for a one-bedroom ADU and 1,000 sq ft for a unit with two or more bedrooms - so even where a local ordinance tries to limit size, those state minimums give you room for a real floor plan. In practice, this means a well-designed 1-bedroom or 2-bedroom is protected by law almost everywhere in the Bay Area.

A few other 2026 rules affect the plan you should draw. SB 13 waives impact fees on any ADU under 750 sq ft, which is a strong reason to keep a studio or 1-bedroom just under that line. AB 976 removed the owner-occupancy requirement for standard ADUs, so a detached rental no longer forces you to live on-site - but a JADU still requires owner-occupancy under AB 1154, because it shares the lot and often a wall with your home. And AB 1033 now lets some cities allow you to sell an ADU separately, condo-style, which can make a larger, standalone-feeling floor plan worth the extra cost. These laws are covered in full in our complete ADU guide.

Studio vs 1-bedroom vs 2-bedroom layouts

The choice between a studio, 1-bedroom, and 2-bedroom is the core floor-plan decision, and it comes down to three trade-offs: cost, income, and how much of your yard you are willing to give up. Here is how the three standard layouts compare on the factors that matter most.

LayoutTypical sizeBest forBay Area rent (2026)
Studio350-500 sq ftSingle tenant, guest suite, office; lowest build cost~$2,000-$2,500/mo
1-bedroom500-750 sq ftMost versatile rental; couples and long-term tenants~$2,500-$3,000/mo
2-bedroom800-1,200 sq ftFamilies, multigenerational living, top rent~$3,000-$3,500+/mo

A studio is the most efficient use of a small yard and the cheapest to build, but its open plan limits it to one occupant and the lowest rent. A 1-bedroom is the sweet spot for most Bay Area homeowners: a separate bedroom makes it a real home, it rents to couples and long-term tenants, and at 500-750 sq ft it can still slip under the 750 sq ft impact-fee line. A 2-bedroom earns the most and suits family or multigenerational use, but it needs the largest footprint, costs the most, and consumes the most yard. Rent figures depend on size, location, and finish; our upcoming ADU rental income guide breaks down what each layout can earn.

How to choose the right ADU floor plan

Start with the lot, not the layout. Measure your buildable footprint after setbacks and existing structures, because that available area - not your dream plan - sets the real ceiling on size. Once you know how much yard you can build on, pick the smallest layout that meets your goal: a studio or 1-bedroom for rental income and simplicity, a 2-bedroom only if you genuinely need the second room for family or higher rent. Overbuilding a plan you do not need is the most common and costly floor-plan mistake.

Then let the rules refine it. Keeping the plan under 750 sq ft waives impact fees under SB 13, which can save thousands and often tips a decision toward a well-designed 1-bedroom. If your budget is tight and you have an unused bedroom or attached garage, a JADU under 500 sq ft is the lowest-cost path at roughly $50K-$150K, versus $150K-$450K for a full detached ADU. Finally, think about the future: if you might one day sell the unit separately under AB 1033, a larger, standalone floor plan holds its value better. Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common ADU floor plans and sizes?

The most common California ADU floor plans are the studio (about 350-500 sq ft), the 1-bedroom (about 500-750 sq ft), and the 2-bedroom (about 800-1,200 sq ft). A JADU is capped at 500 sq ft and built inside the existing home, while a detached ADU can go up to 1,200 sq ft.

What is the maximum size for an ADU in California?

California caps a detached ADU at 1,200 square feet, and a JADU at 500 square feet. Cities must also allow at least 850 sq ft for a one-bedroom ADU and 1,000 sq ft for a unit with two or more bedrooms, so those minimums effectively set the floor for your plan.

What is the difference between a JADU and a standard ADU floor plan?

A JADU (junior ADU) is capped at 500 sq ft and must be created within the existing home's walls, so it often reuses a bedroom or attached garage, may share a bathroom with the main house, and requires owner-occupancy under AB 1154. A standard ADU can be detached, larger, and no longer requires owner-occupancy under AB 976.

Should I build a studio, 1-bedroom, or 2-bedroom ADU?

A studio (about 350-500 sq ft) suits a single tenant, guest suite, or home office and costs the least. A 1-bedroom (about 500-750 sq ft) is the most versatile rental. A 2-bedroom (800-1,200 sq ft) fits a family or the highest rent but costs the most and needs the largest yard footprint.

How does ADU size affect cost?

An ADU in San Jose costs roughly $150,000-$450,000 total, or about $300-$500 per square foot, so a larger floor plan raises the total but often lowers the per-square-foot rate. Building an ADU under 750 sq ft also avoids impact fees under SB 13, which can favor a compact plan.

How do I choose the right ADU floor plan for my lot?

Match the plan to the lot and the goal. Confirm your buildable footprint after setbacks, then pick the smallest layout that meets your use - a studio or 1-bedroom for rental income, a 2-bedroom for family. Staying under 750 sq ft waives impact fees, while a JADU under 500 sq ft is the lowest-cost path inside an existing home.
