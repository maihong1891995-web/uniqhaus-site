Do you need a permit for a home addition in Santa Clara County?

Yes - a home addition permit is always required in Santa Clara County, with no like-for-like exemption, because any addition creates new square footage. A remodel can sometimes skip a permit when it swaps a fixture in the same spot, but the moment you add floor area you trigger new foundation, framing, roof, and often electrical and mechanical scope that the building department must review and inspect. That holds whether the project is a small bump-out under 150 square feet, a 200-400 square foot bedroom or family-room addition, a master-suite addition, or a second story.

The reason is straightforward: a permit certifies that new structure is safe, that it meets current energy and building codes, and that it respects your lot's zoning limits. Skipping it risks stop-work orders, fines, and problems at resale when an appraiser or buyer finds unpermitted square footage. What permits and fees an addition requires

Addition permit fees commonly total $3,000-$10,000 or more in Santa Clara County, because an addition bundles several fees a remodel does not. Where a bathroom permit is a single modest building permit, an addition stacks a plan-check fee, the building permit itself, school fees tied to new square footage, and possibly impact fees. The table below shows what typically makes up that total.

FeeWhat it coversNotes
Plan-check feeStaff review of your stamped structural, energy, and site plansCharged up front when you submit; scales with project size
Building permitAuthorization to build plus required inspectionsThe core permit for the added square footage
School feesPer-square-foot fee on new residential floor areaApplies because you are adding conditioned space
Impact feesPossible fees for utilities, traffic, or parks depending on jurisdictionVary by city; may not apply to every project
Typical totalAll of the above combined$3,000-$10,000+

One useful note if your addition is an attached ADU rather than standard living space: under SB 13, California waives impact fees on ADUs under 750 square feet, which can shave the total. That is one reason homeowners considering an in-law suite compare a standard addition against an attached ADU - the fee math differs. The San Jose plan-review and permit process (SJPermits.org)

In San Jose you file your addition at SJPermits.org, the city's online portal, and the permit issues only after your plans clear plan review - a process that runs about 2-4 months for an addition. Unlike an over-the-counter permit for a minor job, an addition goes through full structural plan review, so the quality and completeness of your submittal directly sets how fast you get through it.

The path looks like this:

  • Prepare stamped plans: architectural, structural, energy (Title 24), and a site plan showing setbacks and lot coverage.
  • Submit at SJPermits.org: upload plans and pay the plan-check fee to enter the review queue.
  • Plan review: building, and often planning, staff check the plans against code and zoning; expect one or more correction cycles.
  • Respond to corrections: revise and resubmit - each round adds time, which is why clean, complete plans matter.
  • Pay fees and pull the permit: once approved, remaining fees are due and the permit issues so construction can begin.

As of January 1, 2026, the 2025 California Building Codes are in effect, so plans must meet current structural, energy, and safety standards. A ground-floor addition must respect setbacks and lot-coverage limits, while a second story adds structural review for reinforcement and temporary weatherproofing - which is why second-story projects usually draw more scrutiny. Because a whole-home remodel or addition where one team draws the plans and manages the review keeps correction cycles short, homeowners in San Jose often see a smoother path than when a designer and contractor work separately.

How long do addition permits take?

Plan review for a home addition commonly takes 2-4 months in Santa Clara County - dramatically longer than a remodel permit, which can be days to a few weeks. The gap comes from what is being reviewed: an addition means new foundation and framing, so staff examine structural calculations, energy compliance, and zoning, and typically send back one or more correction cycles that you must answer before approval.

Two things move that number. First, submittal quality: complete, code-current, professionally stamped plans clear faster than thin ones that trigger repeated corrections. Second, project type: a straightforward single-room addition reviews faster than a second-story addition, which layers in structural reinforcement and weatherproofing questions. The practical takeaway is to treat permitting as a real 2-4 month phase in your schedule, not an afterthought - which is exactly how the full timeline below is built.

The full addition timeline, permits included

A home addition takes 4-8 months end to end in the Bay Area, and permitting is the middle 2-4 months of it. The three phases are design, permitting, and construction, and additions run longer than remodels at every stage because there is new foundation, framing, and a full plan review rather than work inside existing walls. The table shows how the phases stack up.

PhaseDurationWhat happens
Design1-2 monthsConcept, layout, structural approach, and construction documents
Permitting2-4 monthsPlan-check submittal, review, corrections, and permit issuance
Construction3-5 monthsFoundation, framing, roof, systems, and finishes with inspections
Total4-8 monthsDesign through final inspection

The phases do not have to run strictly one after another - a design-build team can finalize construction documents while early planning questions are resolved, and can order long-lead materials during plan review so construction starts the moment the permit issues. A second-story addition tends toward the long end of the range (and sometimes a temporary move-out during structural work), while a ground-floor bump-out sits at the shorter end. Frequently Asked Questions

Do you need a permit for a home addition in Santa Clara County?

Yes. A home addition permit is always required in Santa Clara County because you are adding square footage - new walls, foundation, framing, and roof. Unlike a like-for-like remodel, there is no exemption for adding conditioned floor area, so every bump-out, room addition, and second story needs a permit filed with the building department.

How much does a home addition permit cost in Santa Clara County?

Addition permit costs commonly run $3,000-$10,000 or more in Santa Clara County. That total bundles the plan-check fee, the building permit, school fees, and possible impact fees - a bigger bill than a remodel permit because you are creating new square footage rather than replacing finishes.

Where do you file for an addition permit in San Jose?

In San Jose you file at SJPermits.org, the city's online permitting portal. You submit stamped plans for plan review, respond to any correction cycles, pay the fees, and the permit issues once the plans are approved. As of January 1, 2026, plans must meet the 2025 California Building Codes.

How long does addition plan review take?

Plan review for a home addition commonly takes 2-4 months in Santa Clara County. Additions go through full structural plan review - foundation, framing, energy, and often multiple correction cycles - which is why they take far longer to approve than a bathroom or kitchen remodel.

How long does a whole home addition take?

A home addition typically takes 4-8 months end to end: 1-2 months of design, 2-4 months of permitting, and 3-5 months of construction. Additions run longer than remodels because they involve new foundation, framing, and a full plan review rather than working inside existing walls.

Is the permit different for a second-story addition?

A ground-floor addition must respect setbacks and lot-coverage limits and consumes yard area, while a second-story addition preserves the yard but adds structural review for reinforcement and temporary weatherproofing. Both need a full permit; the second story usually draws more plan-review scrutiny and cost.

  • Plan review runs about 2-4 months because additions go through full structural review, far longer than a remodel permit.
