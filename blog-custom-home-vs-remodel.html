Custom Home vs Whole House Remodel: Which Is Right?

A whole house remodel ($100 to $500 per sq ft) is usually faster and cheaper and keeps your California Prop 13 tax basis. Building a custom home ($400 to $700+ per sq ft) delivers a fully new, code-current home but costs more and takes longer. Remodel when the structure is sound and you love the location; build when the home is failing or you want everything new.

Choosing between building a custom home and remodeling the one you have is the biggest fork in any major home project. The short version: a whole house remodel is usually faster and cheaper and protects your property tax basis, while a custom build gives you a fully new home at a higher cost and a longer timeline. The right answer depends on the condition of your existing home, how much you want to change, and how much the Bay Area location matters to you. This guide compares both paths side by side, and it pairs with our building a custom home guide and the whole house remodel service.

Custom home vs whole house remodel

At a high level, a whole house remodel reworks a home you already own while keeping its foundation and much of its structure, and a custom build creates a brand-new home from the ground up. Remodeling costs about $100 to $500 per sq ft and runs roughly 4 to 9 months of construction; a custom home costs about $400 to $700+ per sq ft plus land and takes roughly 12 to 24 months. Here is how the two compare on the factors that decide most projects:

The pattern is consistent: remodeling wins on cost, speed, and tax basis, while building wins on freedom and a completely new result. The deciding question is whether the existing structure is worth keeping.

When a whole house remodel makes sense

A whole house remodel is the right call in most cases where the bones of the home are good. Choose to remodel when:

  • The structure is sound. If the foundation and framing are in good shape, you are reusing the most expensive parts of a house, and your budget goes into the spaces you actually use.
  • You love the location. In the Bay Area, the lot and neighborhood often carry most of the value. Remodeling lets you stay exactly where you want to be.
  • You want to keep your tax basis. A remodel generally preserves your California Prop 13 base year value, and only the added value of the new work is assessed on top - a meaningful saving versus a full reassessment.
  • You want it done sooner. At roughly 4 to 9 months of construction, a remodel is far faster than a ground-up build.

For real numbers by scope and home size, see our whole house remodel cost guide. Remodeling is the value play when the shell is worth keeping.

When building a custom home makes sense

Building a custom home is the better choice when the existing house is holding you back more than it is helping. Build when:

  • The structure is failing. A cracked or sinking foundation, widespread rot, or framing that cannot support the changes you want can cost so much to fix that new construction is the smarter spend.
  • You have a teardown lot. If the existing home is small, dated, or in poor condition on a good lot, clearing it and building new can unlock the site's full value.
  • You want a fully new home or a different footprint. When your vision needs a new layout, more floors, or a footprint the current house cannot reach, a custom build gives you a blank slate and a code-current result.

A custom build costs about $400 to $700+ per sq ft plus land and takes roughly 12 to 24 months, so it is a larger commitment. Our building a custom home guide and custom homes service walk through the full process.

Cost comparison

Cost is where the two paths separate most clearly. A whole house remodel runs about $100 to $500 per sq ft, depending on how deep the scope goes and how high the finishes are. Building a custom home runs about $400 to $700+ per sq ft, and that figure does not include the cost of land, which in the Bay Area is often the single largest line item.

The gap comes from what each path reuses. A remodel keeps the foundation, framing, site work, and utility connections you have already paid for, so your money concentrates on the spaces being changed. A custom build pays for all of that from scratch - excavation, foundation, full framing, new systems, and site development - before a single finish goes in. For a detailed breakdown of new-build costs, see our cost to build a custom home guide. As a rule of thumb, if the existing structure is sound, remodeling delivers more finished home per dollar; if it is not, that advantage shrinks fast.

The middle ground: major remodel or teardown-rebuild

Many projects do not land cleanly at either extreme. A major remodel can take a home nearly down to the studs, add square footage, and rework the layout while keeping the foundation and enough of the structure to preserve the existing tax basis. A teardown-rebuild removes the old house entirely and builds new on the same lot, giving you a fully new home while keeping the location you chose.

The tipping point is usually cost and condition: once fixing and working around the old structure approaches the cost of building new, a rebuild starts to make sense. Our remodel vs rebuild guide walks through exactly where that line falls and how to price both options.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to build or remodel?

Remodeling is usually cheaper. A whole house remodel runs about $100 to $500 per square foot, while building a custom home runs about $400 to $700 or more per square foot and does not include the cost of land. Remodeling reuses the existing foundation, framing, and site work, which is where much of a new build's cost goes.

Should I build a custom home or remodel?

Remodel when the structure is sound and you love the location, because it is usually faster and cheaper and it keeps your California Prop 13 tax basis. Build a custom home when the existing structure is failing, the layout cannot be fixed, or you want a fully new, code-current home. The condition of the home and how much you want to change decide it.

Does building a new home reset my property taxes?

A remodel generally keeps your existing Prop 13 base year value, and only the added value of the new construction is assessed on top. A full teardown and rebuild is treated as new construction and can trigger a larger reassessment of the improvements. Property tax questions are specific to your situation, so confirm the details with the Santa Clara County Assessor.

How long does building vs remodeling take?

A whole house remodel typically takes about 4 to 9 months of construction after design and permitting, while building a custom home usually takes about 12 to 24 months from design through move-in. Permitting with the local building department is the variable that moves both schedules the most.

When is a teardown-rebuild better than a remodel?

A teardown and rebuild makes more sense when the foundation or framing is failing, when the cost to fix the existing structure approaches the cost of new construction, or when the layout and footprint you want cannot be reached by remodeling. If the shell is sound, a whole house remodel almost always delivers more value for the money.

Key Takeaways

  • A whole house remodel ($100 to $500 per sq ft) is usually faster and cheaper than a custom build ($400 to $700+ per sq ft plus land).
  • Remodeling generally keeps your California Prop 13 tax basis; a full teardown-rebuild is treated as new construction and can trigger a larger reassessment.
  • Remodel when the structure is sound and you love the location; build when the home is failing, the lot is a teardown, or you want a fully new footprint.
  • Once fixing the old structure approaches the cost of building new, a major remodel or teardown-rebuild starts to make more sense.
