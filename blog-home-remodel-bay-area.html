How much does a whole-home remodel cost in the Bay Area?

A whole-home remodel in the Bay Area costs about $100-$200 per sq ft for a cosmetic refresh, $200-$350 per sq ft for a mid-range remodel, and $350-$500+ per sq ft for a full gut in 2026. For a typical 1,500-2,500 sq ft home that is roughly $150,000 to $600,000+, and a full gut of a larger home passes $1,000,000. Plan on 6 to 12 months from first design meeting to move-in, and hold a 10-20% contingency.

Key Takeaways

  • Three price levels: cosmetic refresh $100-$200/sq ft, mid-range $200-$350/sq ft, full gut $350-$500+/sq ft.
  • Depth of work moves the number more than square footage: moving walls, plumbing, and electrical is what pushes a project into the next level.
  • The kitchen (20-30% of the budget) and bathrooms (10-20%) are the most expensive square footage in the house.
  • Budget 20-30% on top of construction for design and permits (8-15%) and contingency (10-20%), plus temporary housing if you move out.
  • Total time is about 6-12 months: 2-3 months of design and permitting, then 4-9 months of construction for most homes.
  • In the Pacific region, a midrange bath remodel recoups about 91% at resale and a major midrange kitchen about 57% (Cost vs Value 2025), so remodel for how you live, not only for resale.

How much does a whole-home remodel cost in the Bay Area?

A Bay Area whole-home remodel costs about $100 to $500+ per square foot in 2026. On a 1,500-2,500 sq ft home, a light refresh starts around $150,000, a mid-range remodel usually lands between $300,000 and $600,000+, and a full gut of a larger home can pass $1,000,000. In our experience, Bay Area labor and materials run roughly 20-30% above national averages, so national cost calculators tend to come in low here.

Two decisions set most of your number: how much of the existing house you keep, and the grade of finishes you choose. If the layout, walls, and systems stay where they are, you are near the low end. If the house goes down to the studs and gets a new floor plan, you are near the top. The rest of this guide breaks that range into pieces you can plan around.

Cost by renovation scope

Scope is the biggest cost lever. The same 2,000 sq ft house can cost $200,000 or $1,000,000 depending on how deep the work goes. Here is how the three levels compare on common Bay Area home sizes.

ScopeCost / sq ft1,500 sq ft home2,000 sq ft home2,500 sq ft homeTotal timeline
Cosmetic refresh$100-$200$150K-$300K$200K-$400K$250K-$500K3-4 months

Full gut renovation$350-$500+$525K-$750K+$700K-$1M+$875K-$1.25M+9-12+ months

Timelines include design and permitting. Totals are construction plus design, before contingency and temporary housing, which we cover below.

Cosmetic refresh ($100-$200 per sq ft)

A refresh keeps the floor plan, the walls, and most of the wiring and plumbing where they are. The money goes into the surfaces you see and touch every day. It is the right level when the house works well but looks tired.

  • New flooring, interior paint, trim, and doors
  • Kitchen and bath updates in the same footprint: cabinet fronts or new boxes, counters, fixtures
  • New lighting, switches, and outlets on existing circuits
  • Often no structural permit, though plumbing and electrical changes still need one

Mid-range remodel ($200-$350 per sq ft)

This is where most Bay Area whole-home projects land. The kitchen and bathrooms are fully rebuilt, a wall or two comes out to open the main living area, and the systems behind the walls get updated where they are touched.

  • Full kitchen and bath remodels, including moved fixtures
  • Selective layout changes, often an open kitchen, living, and dining area
  • Electrical panel upgrade and partial rewiring or repiping
  • New windows, insulation where walls are open, and Title 24 energy compliance

Full gut renovation ($350-$500+ per sq ft)

A gut takes the house down to the frame and rebuilds it with a new plan. It suits older homes where the layout, systems, and structure all need work at the same time. Our gut renovation cost guide covers this level in depth.

  • Re-planned layout with structural changes, beams, and engineering
  • Complete rewire, repipe, new HVAC, and often a panel or service upgrade
  • Seismic and foundation work where the engineer requires it
  • Premium finishes, custom cabinetry, and new exterior envelope work

What changes the cost from city to city

A whole-home remodel uses the same trades in San Jose and Palo Alto, so the base rate per square foot is similar across the South Bay and Peninsula. What changes is the local review process, the housing stock, and the site rules that apply to your lot. Those add design time, fees, and sometimes scope. Where the budget goes

On a mid-range whole-home remodel, the money is not spread evenly across the house. Wet rooms and the systems behind the walls take most of it, while bedrooms and living areas cost far less per square foot.

Budget areaTypical share of budgetWhat drives it
Kitchen20-30%Cabinetry, counters, appliances, moved plumbing and gas
Bathrooms10-20%Waterproofing, tile, fixtures; scales with the number of baths
Structural and framing10-20%Removed bearing walls, beams, foundation and seismic work
Electrical, plumbing, HVAC15-25%Rewire, repipe, panel upgrade, new heating and cooling
Finishes, flooring, paint15-25%The visible layer across every room
Design, engineering, permits8-15%Architecture, 3D design, structural and energy calcs, city fees

Kitchen (20-30% of the budget)

A mid-range kitchen in San Jose runs about $55,000-$85,000, and it climbs fast once you move the sink or range, add an island with plumbing, or choose custom cabinetry. See our kitchen remodel cost guide for line items.

Bathrooms (10-20% of the budget)

A mid-range bathroom runs about $30,000-$55,000 each in San Jose. A primary bath with a curbless shower, double vanity, and heated floor costs more. Details are in our bathroom remodel cost guide.

Structural work (10-20% of the budget)

Opening the main floor usually means removing a bearing wall and adding a beam, which needs a structural engineer and a permit. Older homes may also need foundation or seismic upgrades once the walls are open. Read more about the cost of removing walls and seismic retrofits during a remodel.

Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC (15-25% of the budget)

Homes built before about 1980 often have undersized panels, ungrounded or aluminum wiring, and galvanized pipe. A whole-home remodel is the cheapest time to fix them, because the walls are already open. See rewiring and repiping a whole house and panel upgrades for an all-electric remodel.

Finishes and materials (15-25% of the budget)

Flooring, tile, paint, doors, trim, and lighting are where personal taste shows up in the price. The same layout can swing by tens of thousands of dollars on finish choices alone, which is why we lock selections before pricing. Our guide on choosing remodel finishes helps you decide where to spend.

Design, engineering, and permits (8-15% of the budget)

Architecture, interior design, structural engineering, Title 24 energy calculations, and city fees together run about 8-15% of the project. Skipping design to save money is the most common reason budgets fail later, because an incomplete plan cannot be priced accurately. See what permit drawings cost.

What demolition can uncover

On a house built before 1980, opening the walls can reveal problems that were invisible during the walkthrough:

  • Asbestos in old flooring, duct wrap, or popcorn ceilings, and lead in old paint, which need testing and licensed abatement
  • Knob-and-tube or aluminum branch wiring
  • Galvanized supply lines and cracked cast-iron drains
  • Dry rot and termite damage in sill plates and framing
  • Missing insulation, which Title 24 may require you to add once walls are open

Inspecting these areas during design, and pricing the likely fixes up front, shrinks the surprise. Our hidden costs guide covers each one in detail.

Temporary housing

Most full-gut and many mid-range remodels require you to move out for part or all of construction. A short-term rental in the Bay Area runs about $3,000-$6,000 a month, and an extended-stay hotel costs more. On a 6-month build, that alone can be $18,000-$36,000. See should you move out during a remodel.

Storage and moving

Furniture has to go somewhere while floors and walls are redone. Budget for a storage unit for the full construction period plus two moves, out and back.

Landscaping and site repair

Dumpsters, material deliveries, and equipment damage lawns and planting beds, and new utility trenches cut through the yard. If outdoor work is not in your scope, set money aside to repair the site at the end.

How to budget for a whole-home remodel

A reliable budget is built in three steps, in this order. Skipping the first one is how most projects end up overpaying for change orders later.

Step 1: Define your scope honestly

Write down what has to change, what you would like to change, and what can wait. Then confirm the condition of the house before you set a number.

  • List the must-have changes: layout, kitchen, baths, systems
  • Get an inspection of wiring, plumbing, roof, and foundation on an older home
  • Decide whether you will live in the house during construction
  • Pick a finish level for each room, not one for the whole house

Step 2: Build the budget with contingency

Construction is only part of the total. A complete whole-home budget usually runs 20-30% above the construction price.

  • Construction: the scope and finish level from the tables above
  • Design, engineering, and permits: 8-15%
  • Contingency: 10-20%, higher on homes built before 1980
  • Temporary housing and storage if you move out
  • Furniture, window coverings, and appliances not in the contract

Our home remodel budget guide walks through the allocation line by line.

Step 3: Compare bids on the same scope

Three bids only help if they price the same work. Give every contractor the same drawings and finish list, and ask for itemized pricing rather than one lump sum. Watch for low allowances, missing permits or engineering, and vague exclusions. In California, the down payment on a home improvement contract is capped at $1,000 or 10% of the contract price, whichever is less, so a large deposit request is a red flag (CSLB). Whole-home remodel timeline

A whole-home remodel in the Bay Area takes about 6 to 12 months from the first design meeting to move-in. Roughly 2-3 months go to design and permitting before any wall comes down.

PhaseCosmetic refreshMid-range remodelFull gut
Design and permitting1-2 months2-3 months2-3+ months
Construction6-10 weeks4-6 months7-10 months
Total3-4 months6-9 months9-12+ months

Two things stretch the schedule most: city plan review comments, and long-lead materials such as custom cabinetry and windows. Ordering those during permitting, not after, keeps construction from stalling. ROI: does a whole-home remodel pay for itself?

Usually not dollar for dollar. Overall, a whole-home remodel recoups roughly 50-70% of its cost at resale, and the smaller, targeted projects return the most. The national Cost vs Value Report shows the pattern clearly for the Pacific region, which includes the Bay Area:

Bath remodel, upscale$86,86344.5%
Major kitchen remodel, upscale$171,95038.8%
Primary suite addition, midrange$185,43132.2%

Source: Cost vs Value Report 2025, Pacific region (Zonda / JLC). Bay Area job costs typically run higher than these regional averages.

The takeaway: resale return falls as projects get bigger and more custom. If you plan to sell within a few years, a well-designed mid-range remodel is the safer bet. If you plan to stay ten years or more, the real return is a house that fits how you live, plus keeping your Prop 13 tax base instead of buying a more expensive home. Financing a whole-home remodel

Most Bay Area homeowners fund a whole-home remodel with the equity already in the house. The common options in California are:

  • HELOC or home equity loan: borrow against existing equity, often the lowest-cost option for long-time owners.
  • Cash-out refinance: replace your mortgage with a larger one, which makes sense mainly when the new rate is close to your current one.
  • Renovation loans: FHA 203(k) and Fannie Mae HomeStyle lend against the after-renovation value.
  • Construction-to-permanent loans: funds release in draws during construction, then convert to a standard mortgage.

On a long project, ask how funds are released. A draw schedule tied to construction milestones protects you and keeps the work moving. Compare the options in our California remodel financing guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a whole-home remodel cost in the Bay Area in 2026?

About $100-$200 per sq ft for a cosmetic refresh, $200-$350 for a mid-range remodel, and $350-$500+ for a full gut. For a 1,500-2,500 sq ft home that is roughly $150,000 to $600,000+, and a full gut of a larger home can pass $1,000,000.

How much does it cost to remodel a 2,000 sq ft house in the Bay Area?

A 2,000 sq ft house costs about $200,000-$400,000 for a cosmetic refresh, $400,000-$700,000 for a mid-range remodel, and $700,000 to $1,000,000+ for a full gut, before contingency and temporary housing.

How long does a whole-home remodel take in the Bay Area?

About 6 to 12 months from the first design meeting to move-in: roughly 2-3 months of design and permitting, then 4-9 months of construction. A cosmetic refresh can finish in 3-4 months, and a full gut can run past 12 months.

What is the most expensive part of a whole-home remodel?

The kitchen, which usually takes 20-30% of the budget, followed by the electrical, plumbing, and HVAC systems at 15-25% and the bathrooms at 10-20%. A mid-range San Jose kitchen runs about $55,000-$85,000.

How do I avoid cost overruns on a whole-home remodel?

Finish the design and finish selections before you approve a price, inspect an older home's wiring, plumbing, and foundation during design, insist on itemized pricing, and hold a 10-20% contingency. Changes made during construction cost the most.

Should I gut my house or do a cosmetic remodel?

Gut the house if the layout, systems, and structure all need work, because opening the walls once is cheaper than doing it twice. Choose a cosmetic or mid-range remodel if the floor plan works and the systems are in good shape.

Can I live in my home during a whole-home remodel?

Sometimes, during a cosmetic refresh or a phased remodel. For a mid-range remodel with kitchen and bath work, or any full gut, most families move out for part or all of construction. Budget about $3,000-$6,000 a month for a Bay Area short-term rental.

Do you need permits for a whole-home remodel?

Yes, for any structural, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, or layout change. San Jose permits are filed through SJPermits.org, and since January 1, 2026 plans must meet the 2025 California building and energy codes.

Is a whole-home remodel worth it in the Bay Area?

Usually, if you plan to stay. A whole-home remodel recoups roughly 50-70% of its cost at resale, but it lets long-time owners keep their location and Prop 13 tax base instead of buying a more expensive home. In the Pacific region, a midrange bath remodel recoups about 91% and a major midrange kitchen about 57% (Cost vs Value 2025).
