How Long Does a Bathroom Remodel Take? (2026 Timeline by Phase)

A bathroom remodel in San Jose takes 8-14 weeks from design to finished room: 2-3 weeks design, 2-4 weeks permitting, 3-6 weeks tile and fixture lead time, and only 3-5 weeks of active construction. Tile lead time, waterproofing cure time, and inspections - not demolition - set the clock.

Most homeowners picture a bathroom remodel as a few weeks of demolition and tile. The reality is longer and, once you see the breakdown, far more predictable: a San Jose bathroom remodel takes 8-14 weeks from design to finished room, and the crews are only in your house for 3-5 of those weeks. The rest is design, permitting, and - the single most underestimated line - waiting for tile and fixtures to arrive. This guide maps the full schedule phase by phase, walks through the actual build week by week, explains what causes delays, and shows how running everything under one roof compresses the calendar. It is the timeline companion to our San Jose bathroom remodel guide.

How long does a bathroom remodel take in San Jose?

A San Jose bathroom remodel takes 8-14 weeks end to end, and the number that surprises people is that active construction is only 3-5 weeks of that total. The other 5-9 weeks are front-loaded planning: 2-3 weeks of design, 2-4 weeks of permitting, and 3-6 weeks of tile and fixture lead time before a single wall comes down. In other words, the demolition and building most people worry about is the shortest phase of the project.

Where you land in the 8-14 week range depends mostly on three things: whether your project needs plan review (which adds 2-4 weeks to permitting), how long your selected tile and fixtures take to arrive, and whether you are moving plumbing. A like-for-like guest bath with in-stock materials and no plan review lands near the 8-week end; a master suite with relocated fixtures, custom slabs, and a full plan check runs closer to 14. The good news is that the two longest phases - permitting and material lead time - can run at the same time if the project is managed as one process rather than a series of handoffs.

Timeline by phase - design, permit, lead time, construction

The 8-14 week total breaks into four phases, and only the last one involves crews in your home. The table below shows each phase and its typical duration for a San Jose bathroom in 2026.

PhaseDurationWhat happens
Design2-3 weeksLayout, fixture and tile selections, 3D visualization, and a fixed scope and budget
Permitting2-4 weeksFile online at SJPermits.org; ~1-3 weeks to issue, +2-4 weeks if plan review is required
Tile & fixture lead time3-6 weeksOrdering and receiving tile, vanity, tub, shower, and fixtures - often the longest single phase
Active construction3-5 weeksDemo, rough-in, waterproofing, tile, and finish - the only phase with crews on site
Total8-14 weeksDesign to finished, inspected room

The key insight is that these phases overlap when one team runs the project. Design finishes, the permit is filed, and long-lead tile and fixtures are ordered all in the same window - so materials are arriving as the permit clears, and construction can begin the moment both are ready. Permit fees in San Jose run $400-$1,500, and a permit is required whenever you move plumbing or electrical, change the layout, or alter walls; like-for-like swaps in the same spots are often exempt. Week-by-week: what happens during the 3-5 week build

Once materials are on site and the permit is issued, active construction runs 3-5 weeks in a fixed sequence, and each step has to cure or pass inspection before the next begins. Here is how a typical San Jose bathroom build unfolds day by day.

StageDurationWhat happens
Demolition1-2 daysStrip the room to studs and subfloor; haul out old tile, vanity, tub, and fixtures
Rough-in plumbing & electrical3-5 daysSet new supply and drain lines, shower valves, wiring, exhaust fan; rough inspection before walls close
Waterproofing & tile1-2 weeksMembrane, backer board, then tile - cure time between waterproofing coats is the pacing item
Vanity, fixtures & finish~1 weekInstall vanity, toilet, shower glass, lighting, paint, and final fixtures; final inspection

Demolition is fast - often a single day for a compact bath. The rough-in stage is where a permitted project pauses for its first inspection, because inspectors need to see the plumbing and wiring before drywall hides it. Then comes the phase you cannot rush: waterproofing and tile. Waterproofing membranes need cure time between coats, and setting and grouting tile is slow, precise work - this stretch alone is 1-2 weeks. Finish work - the vanity, toilet, shower glass, and lighting - wraps in about a week, followed by the final inspection that clears the room for use. If you are choosing between a walk-in shower and keeping a tub, that decision affects both the tile scope and this schedule; our main bathroom guide covers the trade-off.

What causes bathroom remodel delays?

Nearly every bathroom remodel that runs long stalls in one of four places, and none of them is the actual building. Knowing them in advance is how you plan around them.

  • Tile and fixture lead time. This is the number-one delay. Ordering materials after demolition instead of during design can add 3-6 weeks of dead time with an open, unusable bathroom. Long-lead items should be on order before the first wall comes down.
  • Waterproofing cure time. Membranes must fully cure between coats before tile can be set - a step that cannot be compressed without risking failure later. Build it into the schedule rather than fighting it.
  • Permit plan review. A straightforward permit issues in about 1-3 weeks, but if your project triggers plan review, add another 2-4 weeks. Layout changes and moved plumbing are the usual triggers.
  • Hidden damage and moved plumbing. Demolition in older San Jose homes can reveal water damage, dated wiring, or corroded pipe behind the walls. And relocating a shower, tub, or toilet adds rough-in work plus another inspection - $3K-$8K and extra days on the calendar. Keeping the existing layout is the single biggest schedule and budget control.

Most of these are avoidable with sequencing, not luck. The projects that finish on the 8-week end share the same habits: materials ordered early, layout kept where possible, and permitting filed the moment design is locked.

How a design-build team shortens the timeline

A design-build studio can shave weeks off a bathroom schedule by overlapping the phases that a designer-plus-separate-contractor arrangement runs one after another. When the same team designs the room, orders the tile and fixtures, and files the permit, those three tracks happen in parallel - so 3-6 weeks of material lead time runs inside the 2-4 week permit window instead of after it.

The bigger time savings is the absence of handoffs. In a fragmented project, the designer finishes and hands off to a contractor, who re-measures, re-prices, and re-orders - each pause costing days or weeks. Under one roof, the whole scope moves as a single schedule. Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a bathroom remodel take in San Jose?

About 8-14 weeks from design to a finished room in San Jose: 2-3 weeks design, 2-4 weeks permitting, 3-6 weeks tile and fixture lead time, and 3-5 weeks of active construction. Active building is the shortest phase.

What is the longest part of a bathroom remodel timeline?

The tile and fixture lead time - 3-6 weeks to order and receive materials - is usually the longest single phase, alongside a 2-4 week permit review. Active construction is only 3-5 weeks. Waterproofing cure time and inspections set the pace inside that window.

How long does the actual construction take?

The active build is 3-5 weeks: demolition 1-2 days, rough-in plumbing and electrical 3-5 days, waterproofing and tile 1-2 weeks with cure time between coats, then vanity, fixtures, and finishing about 1 week. Inspections happen between phases.

What causes bathroom remodel delays?

The most common delays are long tile and fixture lead times, waterproofing cure time that cannot be rushed, permit plan review, and hidden damage found during demolition. Moving plumbing also adds rough-in work and another inspection to the schedule.

How does a design-build team shorten the timeline?

By overlapping phases. A design-build team designs, orders long-lead tile and fixtures, and files the permit in parallel rather than in sequence, so materials arrive as the permit clears and construction can start immediately - often trimming weeks off a handoff-heavy schedule.

How can I speed up my bathroom remodel?

Keeping the existing plumbing layout is the fastest path - moving a shower, tub, or toilet adds rough-in work and another inspection. Ordering tile and fixtures during design, before demolition, removes the single biggest source of mid-project delay.

Key Takeaways

  • A San Jose bathroom remodel takes 8-14 weeks end to end, but active construction is only 3-5 weeks of that total.
  • The schedule is set by tile and fixture lead time (3-6 weeks), waterproofing cure time, and inspections - not demolition, which takes 1-2 days.
  • Permitting runs 2-4 weeks (1-3 weeks to issue, plus 2-4 more if plan review is required); fees are $400-$1,500 in San Jose.
  • A design-build team overlaps design, permitting, and material ordering to trim weeks off the calendar; moving plumbing ($3K-$8K) is the biggest schedule-adder to avoid.
