The single most common question homeowners ask before a project is how long the building permit will take - and the honest answer is that it depends entirely on scope. A like-for-like fixture swap can clear the counter in a day, while a two-story addition can sit in review for months. In San Jose you apply through the city portal at SJPermits.org, and the review path splits sharply between simple over-the-counter work and anything that is structural or adds square footage. This guide maps realistic 2026 timelines by project type, explains what actually causes the delays, and shows how a design-build team can compress the calendar. It is a spoke of our design-build vs general contractor hub, and pairs closely with our step-by-step building permit process walkthrough.

How long do building permits take in San Jose?

Building permits in San Jose range from same-day to about four months, and the deciding factor is whether your project needs plan review. Simple, like-for-like work that does not change the structure or add square footage can be issued over-the-counter, the same day to about a week. Anything structural or that adds floor area is routed to plan review, commonly 3-8 weeks for a residential project, while additions and new construction typically take 2-4 months from application to issued permit.

Those windows are for the permit itself - the time from applying at SJPermits.org to holding an approved permit in hand - not the construction that follows. It is worth separating the two in your head: permitting is one phase of the larger sequence of design, permitting, site prep, framing, MEP rough-in, drywall, finishes, and final sign-off. Because the 2025 California Building Codes have been in effect since January 1, 2026, plans and fixtures must meet current standards, which is exactly what plan review checks. This is the same logic that separates our custom home builds and architecture-led additions from a simple update - the drawings do more work, so review takes longer.

Over-the-counter vs plan review timelines

Over-the-counter permits are for simple, like-for-like work and are issued the same day to about a week; plan review is for anything structural or that adds square footage and commonly runs 3-8 weeks for residential work. The difference is not the size of the fee - it is whether a plans examiner has to open your drawings, check them against the 2025 California Building Codes, and often send them back for corrections before signing off.

The takeaway is that whether you are over the counter or in plan review is decided by your scope, not your choice - and it is set before you apply. Keeping a remodel like-for-like where the goals allow can save weeks, while any project that is genuinely structural should be planned around the 3-8 week (or longer) review window from the start. What causes permit delays?

The most common cause of a slow permit is not the city - it is incomplete or inconsistent drawings that trigger correction cycles. In plan review, an examiner checks your plan set against code; if something is missing, unclear, or non-compliant, the plans come back with corrections, and the clock effectively pauses until you resubmit. Two or three rounds of corrections can add weeks to an otherwise routine review.

The usual delay drivers on a San Jose project:

  • Incomplete or inconsistent plans - drawings that do not match each other or leave code items unaddressed are the number-one source of correction rounds.
  • Changing the scope mid-review - revising the design after you apply resets the examiner's work and pushes the timeline back.
  • Structural work and added square footage - these always require full plan review, so they cannot use the same-week counter path.
  • Slow responses to corrections - every day a resubmittal sits on your side is a day added to the overall timeline.
  • Projects needing extra approvals - anything beyond a straightforward permit adds review steps and time.

Notice how many of these are within your control. A permit is rarely slow because the city is slow; it is slow because the submittal was not right the first time, or because the scope kept moving. That is why who prepares and manages the drawings matters so much - a point our design-build vs general contractor comparison returns to.

How to speed up your permit

The fastest permit is a complete, code-compliant submittal that does not change after you apply. Because most delays come from correction cycles and mid-review scope changes, the levers that actually move the timeline are all about getting the drawings right the first time and keeping the scope fixed once the application is in. In California the contractor - not the owner - should pull the permits, which keeps the submittal and the build in the same hands.

What consistently shortens the calendar:

  • Submit a complete plan set the first time - drawings that address every code item up front avoid the biggest source of delay.
  • Lock the scope before applying - decide the layout and finishes so you are not revising plans mid-review.
  • Respond to corrections immediately - treat any resubmittal as top priority to keep the clock running.
  • Have the contractor pull the permits - under California practice the contractor pulls permits, and a firm that files often knows what the city expects.
  • Overlap design and permitting - a design-build team can start permit prep while design is finishing, compressing the overall schedule.

That last point is where the delivery model matters most. When one team handles design, permitting, and construction, the drawings are built to be permit-ready and the phases overlap instead of waiting on handoffs between separate firms. Our permitting service and architecture team are set up around exactly that, and homeowners across San Jose and neighboring Cupertino benefit from a submittal process the city already knows.

How long does an over-the-counter permit take in San Jose?

An over-the-counter permit for simple, like-for-like work is issued the same day to about a week. It applies to jobs that do not change the structure or add square footage - swapping fixtures in place, minor electrical or plumbing, and similar work.

How long does plan review take in San Jose?

Residential plan review in San Jose commonly takes 3-8 weeks. Anything structural, or that adds square footage, needs plan review, where city staff check the drawings against the 2025 California Building Codes before a permit is issued.

How long does a permit for an addition or new home take?

Additions and new construction typically take 2-4 months from application to issued permit. These projects add square footage and are structural, so they go through full plan review, often with more than one round of corrections.

What causes building permit delays in San Jose?

The most common causes are incomplete or inconsistent drawings, multiple correction cycles during plan review, changing the scope mid-review, and projects that need extra approvals. Each round of corrections resets the clock while the city waits for a resubmittal.

How can I speed up my building permit in San Jose?

Submit complete, code-compliant drawings the first time, keep the scope fixed once you apply, respond to corrections quickly, and have the contractor pull the permits. A design-build team that overlaps design and permitting typically moves through review faster.
