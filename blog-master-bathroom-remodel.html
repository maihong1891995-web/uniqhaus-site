The master bathroom is where a remodel budget goes from practical to personal. It is the largest, most fixture-heavy bath in the home, and the primary-suite tier - $55,000 to $100,000+ in the Bay Area, with a large suite topping $120,000 - buys a room built around how you actually start and end the day: a double vanity so two people are never waiting, a curbless custom shower, often a freestanding tub, heated floors underfoot, and sometimes a full wet room. This guide is the deep dive on that top tier. If you want the whole-market overview first, start with our Bathroom Remodel in San Jose 2026 guide; for a line-by-line breakdown of every tier, see how much a bathroom remodel costs in San Jose.

How much does a master bathroom remodel cost in the Bay Area?

A master bathroom remodel in the Bay Area costs $55,000 to $100,000+ in 2026, and a large primary suite with a wet room and premium slabs can exceed $120,000. On a per-square-foot basis that is still the same $400-$700 range as any Bay Area bathroom - roughly 20-30% above the national average - but a master bath simply has more square footage and more fixtures to spread that rate across. The number climbs because you are buying a bigger room, not because the tile suddenly costs more.

Three choices move a master budget more than anything else, exactly as they do in a smaller bath: whether you keep the existing plumbing layout, the tile and fixture grade, and how many luxury systems you add. Relocating a shower, tub, or toilet means opening walls and floors and re-routing supply and drain lines - often $3,000-$8,000 on its own - which is why keeping the existing fixture locations is the single most effective way to control a master budget. A master suite pushes toward the top of - and past - these dollar figures mainly through the fixtures, vanity, and tile lines: two vanities instead of one, a larger tiled shower, a freestanding tub, and heated-floor mats all land in those categories. What is included in a master vs standard bathroom?

The difference between a master and a standard bath is scope: a guest or hall bath ($15K-$30K) keeps a single vanity and one tub-shower in a compact footprint on the existing layout, while a master suite ($55K-$100K+) adds a second vanity, a separate custom shower and tub, and comfort systems like heated floors. The table below shows what typically comes with each, so you can see exactly where the money goes.

FeatureStandard / guest bath ($15K-$30K)Master / primary suite ($55K-$100K+)
VanitySingle vanity, stock or semi-customDouble vanity, often custom, with dedicated storage
Shower & tubOne combined tub-showerSeparate curbless custom shower and freestanding tub
FootprintCompact, same layout keptLarger, often with relocated fixtures or a wet room
TilePorcelain, single accent wallFull-height stone or large-format porcelain, feature walls
Comfort systemsStandard exhaust fanHeated floors, upgraded lighting, sometimes steam or a wet room
PlumbingLike-for-like, same spotsFrequently relocated (+$3K-$8K)

A powder room (half bath, no shower or tub) sits at the bottom of the ladder around $8K-$15K, and a mid-range full bath at $30K-$55K sits between the two columns above. The jump into the master tier is driven less by luxury tile and more by the second vanity, the separate shower-and-tub pairing, and any plumbing you relocate to make the layout work. Master bathroom layout: double vanity, shower, tub placement

Good master-bath layout starts with one rule: keep the fixtures where they are whenever the room already works, because relocating the shower, tub, or toilet adds $3,000-$8,000 and lengthens the schedule. Within that constraint there is still enormous room to redesign - a double vanity, a walk-in shower, and a tub can all be reconfigured against existing wet walls without moving a single drain.

The double vanity is the defining master feature: two sinks with a shared or split counter so morning routines never collide. Plan at least 60 inches of combined width for a true double, or a single long vanity with two basins where space is tight. Place the toilet in its own compartment or the least-visible corner, and put the freestanding tub where it reads as the focal point - under a window or against a feature wall - since it is sculptural, not just functional. The curbless walk-in shower anchors the wet end of the room; a shared glass wall between shower and tub keeps a compact master feeling open. Because a master carries so many fixtures, this is where design-build coordination earns its keep - our home remodeling team models the layout in 3D before demolition so the plumbing rough-in is right the first time.

Which luxury features are worth it (heated floors, wet room, freestanding tub)?

The two upgrades Bay Area homeowners most consistently call worth it are heated floors and a curbless walk-in shower - you feel both every single day, and the curbless shower adds only about $3,000-$8,000 over a like-for-like tub swap while making the room read larger and age in place. The rest of the luxury menu is a matter of how you actually live, not a default upgrade.

  • Heated floors. Electric radiant mats under tile are the highest-satisfaction upgrade per dollar in a master bath, especially over stone, which is cold underfoot. Best installed during the tile phase, so decide early.
  • Curbless walk-in shower. No threshold to step over, easy to clean, and future-proof for aging in place. The $3K-$8K premium over a tub swap is the most defensible luxury spend in the room.
  • Freestanding tub. Worth it if you genuinely take baths; it is a showpiece if you do not. Keep at least one tub somewhere in the home if you plan to sell to families, but a master rarely needs both a tub and a stall unless you use both.
  • Wet room. Enclosing the tub and shower in one fully waterproofed, glassed zone is the most premium move - it is what pushes a large suite past $120K - because it multiplies the tiled, waterproofed area and the cure time that comes with it.

The honest resale note: a mid-range bathroom recoups about 66-74% of its cost nationally (2025 Cost vs. Value), typically higher in high-value Bay Area markets, and a well-designed refresh beats a full luxury gut on return. Build a spa-level master for your own long-term enjoyment, not to out-earn the neighborhood - see how a bath fits a larger plan in our whole-home remodeling service.

Timeline and permits for a master suite remodel

A master suite remodel takes 8-14 weeks from design to finished room, and a larger master lands at the upper end because waterproofing and tile - not demolition - set the clock, and both scale with the tiled area. Active construction is only 3-5 weeks of that; the rest is design, permitting, and fixture lead time running in parallel.

PhaseDuration
Design2-3 weeks
Permitting2-4 weeks
Tile & fixture lead time3-6 weeks
Active construction3-5 weeks
Total8-14 weeks

Inside the construction window, demolition takes 1-2 days, rough-in plumbing and electrical 3-5 days, waterproofing and tile 1-2 weeks (with cure time between coats - longer for a wet room), and vanity, fixtures, and finishing about 1 week. A master almost always needs a permit because it moves plumbing or electrical, changes the layout, or alters walls; a like-for-like swap in the same spots is often exempt, but a true suite rarely qualifies. San Jose permit fees run $400-$1,500, filed at SJPermits.org, with issuance in about 1-3 weeks (add 2-4 weeks if plan review is required). The 2025 California Building Codes have been in effect since January 1, 2026, including water-efficiency rules on toilets, showerheads, and faucets. We pull and manage all of it through our permitting service, so submittals and inspections stay on the calendar. Homeowners in San Jose, Sunnyvale, and Cupertino get the same in-house handling.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a master bathroom remodel cost in the Bay Area?

A master or primary-suite bathroom remodel in the Bay Area runs about $55,000-$100,000+ in 2026, and a large suite with a wet room and premium slabs can exceed $120,000. At roughly $400-$700 per square foot, the larger footprint and luxury fixtures - not the tile alone - drive the number.

What is the difference between a master and standard bathroom remodel?

A master bath is larger and carries more fixtures: a double vanity, a curbless custom shower, often a freestanding tub, heated floors, and sometimes a wet room. A standard guest or hall bath ($15K-$30K) usually keeps a single vanity and one tub-shower in a compact footprint on the existing plumbing layout.

Which master bathroom luxury features are worth it?

Heated floors and a curbless walk-in shower are the two upgrades most Bay Area homeowners say are worth it, because you feel them daily and they age in place. A curbless shower adds about $3,000-$8,000 over a like-for-like tub swap. A freestanding tub is worth it if you use baths; keep at least one tub in the home for family resale.

Do I need a permit for a master bathroom remodel?

Yes. A master remodel almost always moves plumbing or electrical, changes the layout, or alters walls, so it needs a permit. San Jose bathroom permit fees run $400-$1,500, filed at SJPermits.org. The 2025 California Building Codes have been in effect since January 1, 2026, with water-efficiency rules on toilets, showerheads, and faucets.

How long does a master bathroom remodel take?

About 8-14 weeks from design to finished room: 2-3 weeks design, 2-4 weeks permitting, 3-6 weeks tile and fixture lead time, and 3-5 weeks of active construction. A larger master suite with more tile, a wet room, and heated floors tends to land at the upper end because waterproofing and cure time scale with the tiled area.

What drives up the cost of a master bathroom remodel?

The biggest cost driver is moving plumbing to relocate the shower, tub, or toilet, which adds about $3,000-$8,000 on its own. Keeping the existing fixture locations is the number-one way to control a master bathroom budget; the tile and fixture grade is the next-largest lever.
