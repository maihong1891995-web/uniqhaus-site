How Much Does an Interior Designer Cost for a Remodel?

Interior designers usually charge hourly, a flat fee, a percentage of construction cost, or cost-plus on purchases. National guides put hourly rates at about $100 to $200 and percentage fees at 10% to 30% of construction cost (Forbes Home). On a remodel, the bigger cost lever is how well design and construction are coordinated, which is why many Bay Area owners use a design-build team with in-house interior design.

If you are planning a kitchen or whole-house remodel in San Jose or anywhere in the Bay Area, interior design fees depend far more on how a designer charges than on a single "going rate." National cost guides put typical hourly rates at $100 to $200, and percentage-based fees at 10% to 30% of construction cost, according to Forbes Home. This guide explains each fee model, what an interior designer actually does on a remodel, how design choices move your construction budget, and when a design-build team with in-house interior design is the simpler path.

How do interior designers charge for a remodel?

Most interior designers use one of five fee models, and many combine two of them. The model shapes your total cost, how predictable it is, and whether the designer has any reason to steer your budget up or down. The figures below come from national guides, not Bay Area surveys, so treat them as a starting point for questions rather than a quote.

Two things matter more than the headline number. First, get every fee in writing, including markups on furniture and fixtures. Second, check whether the fee covers construction-phase work, like answering contractor questions and walking the site. A low design fee that stops at a mood board can leave you paying for those hours later.

What does an interior designer do on a remodel, versus decorating?

On a remodel, an interior designer shapes the space before walls close, not just the furniture after. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics describes the job as determining space needs, sketching preliminary plans including electrical and partition layouts, estimating costs, ordering materials, and coordinating with architects, engineers, and general contractors. Designers also need to read and edit blueprints and know building codes and accessibility standards (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook).

Decorating is a different job. A decorator works with finished surfaces: furniture, rugs, art, window treatments, and paint. On a kitchen or whole-house remodel, the decisions that cost the most are made earlier:

  • Layout. Where the sink, range, and island go, and whether a wall comes out.
  • Lighting and electrical plan. Fixture locations, switching, and outlets inside cabinets or islands.
  • Cabinetry design. Sizes, panel layout, interior storage, and how cabinets meet ceilings and windows.
  • Finish and fixture specs. Tile, countertops, plumbing fixtures, and hardware, with model numbers.

Those specs feed straight into the construction price. If you hire a decorator for a remodel, someone else still has to make those calls. Requirements to practice also vary by state. California runs a voluntary certification through the California Council for Interior Design Certification (CCIDC), so it is fair to ask a designer about credentials and past remodel work, not just styling.

How much does an interior designer cost in the Bay Area?

There is no reliable public survey of Bay Area interior design fees, so be wary of any single number. What you can do is test each fee model against your own budget. Kitchen and bath remodels in the Bay Area commonly run $30,000 to $150,000 or more. Using the 10% to 30% percentage range cited by Forbes Home, here is what that model would mean at a few construction budgets:

Construction budgetFee at 10%Fee at 20%Fee at 30%
$75,000 kitchen$7,500$15,000$22,500
$150,000 kitchen and bath$15,000$30,000$45,000
$400,000 whole-house remodel$40,000$80,000$120,000

This is simple arithmetic on a national range, not a Bay Area quote. It shows why the fee model matters: on larger projects, a percentage fee grows quickly, and any change order raises it again. For context on what designers earn rather than bill, the BLS reports a national median wage of $67,190 per year for interior designers as of May 2025 (BLS). Billing rates sit higher than wages because they cover overhead, software, insurance, and unbilled time.

Remember the design fee is only one line. A full budget also includes construction, permits, and a contingency of 10% to 20% for the unknowns behind old walls. Our guide to hidden remodel costs covers the lines owners most often miss.

How do design decisions affect construction cost and change orders?

Design decisions set most of your construction cost, and late decisions are the most common source of owner-driven change orders. A cabinet spec, a tile choice, or a moved sink each changes labor, materials, and sometimes the permit drawings. When those choices are made after the contractor has priced the job, every one becomes a change order.

The handoff is where this usually breaks down. With a standalone designer, the design is often finished before a contractor sees it. The contractor then prices what is drawn, finds details that are expensive to build, and the owner sits in the middle deciding whose advice to follow. Common examples:

  • Custom floor-to-ceiling cabinetry drawn without checking ceiling height variation in an older house.
  • A large-format tile specified on a floor that needs leveling first.
  • Pendant lights placed over an island where the ceiling framing makes boxes or extra work necessary.
  • A wall removal shown on a design board before anyone has checked whether it is load-bearing.

None of these are design mistakes in isolation. They are coordination gaps. The fix is to price design choices while they are still choices. That means the people drawing the space and the people building it work from the same plan, and you lock selections before construction starts. How 3D visualization reduces surprises

A photorealistic 3D model lets you see the room before it is built, so you catch problems on screen instead of on site. You can check sight lines, cabinet proportions, light, and how finishes read together. Many design changes that would be costly mid-construction are free to make in a model. At UniqHaus, our 3D rendering design package is $1 per square foot, and that fee is credited when you build with us. 3D design typically takes about 7 to 10 days, and 2D drawings about 7 days.

Should you hire a standalone interior designer or a design-build team?

Hire a standalone designer if you already have a contractor you trust and want an independent creative voice. Choose a design-build team if you want one contract, one budget, and one point of responsibility from the first sketch to the final build. Both can work well. The difference is who coordinates design, pricing, and construction.

Do you need an interior designer for a kitchen remodel?

You do not need one, but you need someone doing the design work. A kitchen is the densest room in a house: plumbing, gas or induction circuits, ventilation, lighting, appliances, and cabinetry all meet in a small space. Someone has to draw the layout, coordinate appliance specs with cabinet sizes, and plan lighting and outlets before the walls close.

That someone can be an independent designer, a cabinet showroom, or a design-build team. Showroom design is often included with a cabinet purchase, but it usually stops at the cabinets. Ask who handles the lighting plan, the ventilation, and any wall changes. For a refresh with no layout change, a clear finish schedule and a skilled contractor may be enough. Once you are moving the sink, opening a wall, or adding an island, a full design pays for itself in fewer surprises. What does integrated interior design look like on real projects?

Two UniqHaus projects show where interior design and construction overlap most.

  • Hawthorne Ave, Campbell (2025). A whole-home remodel and addition, growing the house from about 1,700 to about 3,117 sq ft with 5 bedrooms and 5 baths. The team reworked the floor plan into an open layout, replaced electrical and plumbing throughout, and added custom floor-to-ceiling cabinetry. Cabinetry at that scale depends on the layout, the electrical plan, and the ceiling all being settled together, which is why we design and build it as one scope.
  • San Jose open-concept remodel. A full home remodel with kitchen and primary bath updates, plus structural changes that removed and repositioned walls to open the living and dining area. Opening walls is where interior design meets engineering: the look you want has to match what the structure allows.

Both projects ran through one design-build team, with architecture, interior design, permits, and construction under one roof. You can see more on our home remodeling page.

What should you ask before you hire anyone for design?

Ask five questions before you sign any design agreement, whether it is with a designer or a design-build firm:

  1. Which fee model do you use, and can I see an estimate of the total design fee in writing?
  2. Do you mark up furniture, fixtures, or trades, and by how much?
  3. When will I see a construction price, before or after the design is final?
  4. Who answers contractor questions and visits the site during construction?
  5. Do you provide 3D views or only 2D plans and boards?

Clear answers to these questions tell you more about your final cost than any hourly rate. At UniqHaus, pricing is itemized, and once you approve it, the price is fixed. Changes you request after approval are priced as change orders, so you always know the cost before you decide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an interior designer charge per hour?

National guides put the average at about $100 to $200 per hour, with a wider range of $50 to $500 depending on experience (Forbes Home). There is no reliable public survey of Bay Area rates, so ask each designer for an hours estimate in writing.

Is an interior designer worth it for a kitchen remodel?

It is worth having someone do real design work once you change the layout, move plumbing, or open a wall. That can be an independent designer or a design-build team. For a cosmetic refresh with the same layout, a clear finish schedule and a skilled contractor may be enough.

Do interior designers mark up furniture and materials?

Many do, especially under cost-plus agreements. Houzz Pro describes cost-plus as usually the most expensive structure for clients. Ask for the markup percentage in writing and whether you will see original invoices.

What is the difference between an interior designer and a decorator?

A decorator works with finished surfaces like furniture, rugs, and paint. An interior designer on a remodel also plans layouts, lighting and electrical locations, and cabinetry, and coordinates with architects and contractors, as the BLS describes the role.

Does a design-build firm include interior design?

Some do and some only handle architecture and construction, so ask. At UniqHaus, interior design, architecture, 3D, permits, and construction are handled by one team, and the 3D rendering design package is $1 per square foot, credited when you build with us.

Can I hire an interior designer and a contractor separately?

Yes, and it works well when you already trust both. The risk is the handoff: if construction is priced after design is finished, conflicts between the drawings and the build tend to surface as change orders, and you end up resolving them between two firms.

Key Takeaways

  • National guides put interior designer fees at about $100 to $200 per hour, or 10% to 30% of construction cost (Forbes Home).
  • A percentage fee grows with your budget: 20% of a $150,000 kitchen and bath is $30,000.
  • Late design decisions are the most common cause of owner-driven change orders, so price choices while they are still choices.
  • UniqHaus 3D rendering design is $1 per sq ft, credited when you build with us; 3D takes about 7 to 10 days.
