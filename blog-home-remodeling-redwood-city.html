Redwood City sits at the heart of the mid-Peninsula, and its homes reflect decades of Bay Area growth, from pre-war cottages near the downtown grid to mid-century ranch neighborhoods and pockets of newer infill. Remodeling a home here is rarely a blank-slate job. It usually means updating an older house to the way people live now while respecting the structure, the systems, and the neighborhood that are already there. This guide covers what home remodeling in Redwood City costs in 2026, how the City of Redwood City permit process works, how long a project takes, and why a design-build approach fits the local housing stock so well. It pairs with our broader home remodeling service and our Bay Area home remodel guide.

Remodeling homes in Redwood City

The first thing to understand about remodeling in Redwood City is how varied the housing stock is. Neighborhoods like Mount Carmel and Roosevelt hold older bungalows and Craftsman-era cottages, the flatlands and Redwood Village areas are dense with mid-century ranch homes from the 1950s and 1960s, and areas closer to the hills and newer subdivisions bring a range of later construction. A single street can mix homes that are seventy years apart in age, and that range drives everything about a remodel.

Older homes carry the issues you would expect from their era. Knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring, galvanized supply plumbing that has narrowed with age, un-permitted additions from previous owners, dated or under-built foundations, and little to no insulation are all common. None of these are dealbreakers, but they change the scope. When you open the walls of a 1920s cottage or a 1955 ranch, you often find work that has to be brought up to current code before the fun finishes go in. That is why a serious remodel plan starts by verifying the existing conditions rather than assuming, because what is behind the walls is what sets the real budget.

The upside is that these homes have good bones and sit on desirable lots. Mid-century ranches in particular respond well to remodeling, since their simple rooflines and single-story footprints make it straightforward to open up interiors, raise ceilings, and connect the house to the yard. The goal in most Redwood City remodels is to keep what makes the home worth owning while quietly rebuilding the parts that no longer serve, and to do it in a way that will pass the City of Redwood City inspections without drama.

Permits for a Redwood City remodel

Most remodels in Redwood City that touch structure or systems require a building permit from the City of Redwood City. The city's building division reviews and permits construction within city limits, and the general rule is simple: purely cosmetic work such as painting, flooring, or swapping a fixture usually does not need a permit, but anything that affects the structure, electrical, plumbing, or mechanical systems does, and a whole-house remodel almost always does.

The process follows a familiar path. You submit drawings and, where required, structural calculations for plan check, the city reviews them for compliance with the current building, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, and energy codes, and the permit is issued once the plans are approved. During construction, the work is verified through a series of inspections at set stages, such as foundation, framing, rough electrical and plumbing, insulation, and final. Larger or more complex projects may involve more than one round of plan-check comments, so building review time into the schedule matters.

Requirements and current fees are set by the City of Redwood City and change over time, so we do not quote specific permit fees here. Confirm what your project needs directly with the City of Redwood City building division, or let your design-build team manage that submission and review for you. Getting the permit set right the first time is one of the biggest levers on the overall timeline, because clean, complete drawings move through plan check faster and avoid the back-and-forth that stalls so many remodels. How long a Redwood City remodel takes

A whole-house remodel in Redwood City is best planned in three phases, and the total calendar is almost always longer than the construction phase alone. Understanding the full arc keeps expectations realistic and prevents the frustration that comes from measuring only the time on the job site.

Design, engineering, and permitting typically takes two to four months. This is where the layout is resolved, structural and systems decisions are made, finishes are selected, and the drawings are prepared for submission. Plan check at the City of Redwood City then adds several weeks to a couple of months, depending on the scope of the project and how many revision cycles the review takes. Construction on a full remodel usually runs four to eight months, with cosmetic projects finishing faster and gut remodels or additions running longer.

Older homes deserve extra schedule contingency. When a 1950s ranch or a pre-war cottage is opened up, hidden conditions like failed wiring, rotted framing, or un-permitted past work regularly surface, and addressing them properly takes time even when the budget allows for it. A team that sequences the work well, orders long-lead items early, and keeps inspections on schedule can hold the timeline tight, but planning for roughly nine to fifteen months from first design meeting to final inspection on a substantial remodel is a healthy expectation.

Why design-build works for a Redwood City remodel

On a Redwood City remodel, a design-build firm reduces risk by putting design, permits, and construction under one team and one contract. Instead of hiring an architect or designer, then bidding the drawings out to a separate general contractor, you work with a single group that is accountable for the whole project from the first sketch through the final City of Redwood City inspection.

That structure matters most on exactly the kind of older, variable housing Redwood City is full of. When the same team designs, prices, permits, and builds the work, the budget is grounded in construction reality from the start, the drawings account for how the house is actually built, and there is no gap between the plan on paper and the crew in the field. It removes the finger-pointing that happens when a separate designer and contractor disagree over what the drawings meant or why the price came in high, and it gives you a single point of accountability if something unexpected turns up behind a wall.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a home remodel cost in Redwood City?

Most whole-house remodels in Redwood City run from roughly 150 to 400 dollars per square foot in 2026, depending on scope and finishes. A cosmetic refresh sits at the low end, a full gut remodel with new kitchen, baths, and systems sits at the high end, and moving walls or adding square footage pushes higher. On a typical 1,600 to 2,000 square foot home that means a broad range from the low six figures to well over half a million dollars.

Do I need a permit to remodel a home in Redwood City?

Yes. Structural work, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, additions, and most whole-house remodels require a building permit from the City of Redwood City, and the work goes through plan check before permits are issued and through inspections during construction. Purely cosmetic work like paint or flooring usually does not need a permit, but anything touching structure or systems does. Confirm current requirements with the City of Redwood City building division.

How long does a home remodel take in Redwood City?

Plan a whole-house remodel in Redwood City in three phases. Design, engineering, and permitting typically take two to four months, plan check at the city adds several weeks to a couple of months depending on scope and revisions, and construction on a full remodel usually runs four to eight months. Older homes often need extra time once walls are open and hidden conditions appear, so build contingency into both the budget and the schedule.

What should I know about remodeling an older Redwood City home?

Redwood City housing is a mix of mid-century ranch homes, older bungalows and cottages, and newer infill, so home age and construction vary widely across neighborhoods. Older homes often carry knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing, un-permitted past additions, and dated foundations that need attention during a remodel. A good remodel plan starts by verifying the existing conditions rather than assuming, because what is behind the walls drives both scope and budget.

Is a design-build firm better for a Redwood City remodel?

For a whole-house remodel, usually yes. A design-build firm puts design, permits, and construction under one team and one contract, so the drawings, the budget, and the crew stay aligned from the first sketch through the final Redwood City inspection. That structure gives you a single point of accountability and a realistic budget early, instead of the gap that opens when a separate designer and general contractor disagree.
