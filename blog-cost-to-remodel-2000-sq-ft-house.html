Two thousand square feet is the size of a typical Bay Area single-family home, which makes it the number most homeowners want a real answer for. The short version: a full remodel of a 2,000 sq ft house usually lands between $200,000 and $1,000,000, driven far more by finish level and scope than by the square footage itself. This guide breaks the number down by level, by room, and by the factors that move it, with the same math we use to price projects across San Jose. It pairs with our whole house remodel cost guide and our cost per square foot breakdown.

How much does it cost to remodel a 2,000 sq ft house?

A full remodel of a 2,000 sq ft house in the Bay Area typically costs $200,000 to $1,000,000. The math is simple: whole-house remodels run about $100 to $500 per square foot, and 2,000 sq ft multiplied across that range gives the spread. Most homeowners doing a genuine whole-house project land in the mid-range band of $200 to $400 per sq ft, or roughly $400,000 to $800,000.

The per-square-foot figure is a planning tool, not a quote. Two 2,000 sq ft homes can differ by hundreds of thousands of dollars depending on how much you keep versus gut, the finishes you choose, and what the walls hide. A light cosmetic update stays near the bottom of the range; a down-to-studs gut with custom finishes pushes past $1,000,000. The sections below show exactly where those differences come from. Cost by finish level

Finish level is the single biggest driver of the total. The table below applies the $100 to $500 per sq ft range to a 2,000 sq ft home so you can place your project on the scale.

LevelCost per sq ft2,000 sq ft total
Cosmetic / refresh$100 - $200/sf$200,000 - $400,000
Mid-range whole-house$200 - $400/sf$400,000 - $800,000
High-end / luxury or full gut$400 - $500+/sf$800,000 - $1,000,000+

A cosmetic refresh keeps the existing layout, cabinets boxes, and systems, and updates surfaces - paint, flooring, countertops, fixtures, and lighting. A mid-range whole-house remodel replaces kitchens and bathrooms, updates finishes throughout, and handles the systems upgrades an older home needs. A high-end or full-gut project takes the house down to the studs, reconfigures the layout, and uses custom materials. A full gut sits at the top of the range - our gut renovation cost guide covers that scenario in detail.

Where the money goes

Within a whole-house budget, the spend is not spread evenly. The kitchen is the largest single line, and design plus permits typically take 8 to 15 percent. The table shows a typical distribution.

AreaTypical share of budgetNotes
Kitchen20 - 30%Largest single line: cabinetry, counters, appliances, plumbing, electrical
Bathrooms10 - 20%High cost per sq ft; scales with number of baths
Structural / framing10 - 20%Foundation, framing, load-bearing changes, additions
Electrical / plumbing / HVAC15 - 25%Rough systems, often a full rewire and repipe on older homes
Finishes / flooring / paint15 - 25%The visible layer across the whole home
Design & permits8 - 15%Architecture, engineering, and City of San Jose permitting

The kitchen and bathrooms carry a disproportionate share because they combine cabinetry, stone, appliances, and wet systems in a small footprint. That is also why two homes of identical size diverge so much: three bathrooms and a chef's kitchen cost far more than one bath and a galley. Systems are the other swing factor - a pre-1980 home that needs a full rewire and repipe spends real money before a single finish goes in.

What drives the cost up or down

Past the headline range, five factors decide where your 2,000 sq ft project actually lands:

  • Finish level. This moves the number most. The same house can cost $200,000 or $1,000,000 depending on whether you refresh surfaces or gut and rebuild with custom materials.
  • Structural and systems condition. Older pre-1980 homes often hide knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing, foundation issues, or missing insulation. What the walls reveal can add tens of thousands once demolition starts.
  • Layout changes. Keeping walls where they are is cheap; moving kitchens, opening up floor plans, or relocating plumbing and load-bearing walls adds structural and systems cost.
  • Permits with the City of San Jose. Whole-house remodels need permits for structural, electrical, plumbing, and layout work, filed at SJPermits.org. Design and permitting run 8 to 15 percent of the budget and set the timeline.
  • Bay Area labor. Skilled-trade rates here are among the highest in the country, which is why the same scope costs more in San Jose than in most of the U.S.

How to budget and avoid surprises

The homeowners who stay on budget do three things before committing a dollar to construction:

  • Get a design and a real bid first. A per-square-foot estimate is for planning; a design and a line-item bid are what you actually build to. Pricing from a finished design is the only way to turn the $200,000 to $1,000,000 range into your number.
  • Hold a 10 to 20 percent contingency. On a $400,000 to $800,000 mid-range remodel, that is roughly $40,000 to $160,000 set aside for what opening the walls reveals - lean toward the high end for older homes.
  • Use one design-build team. When the people who design the project also price and build it, the estimate reflects the real scope, and there are no gaps between an architect's drawing and a builder's bid where the number can jump.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to remodel a 2,000 sq ft house?

Remodeling a 2,000 sq ft house in the Bay Area typically costs $200,000 to $1,000,000, or about $100 to $500 per square foot. A mid-range whole-house remodel at $200 to $400 per sq ft lands around $400,000 to $800,000. Cosmetic refreshes cost less, while a high-end finish or full gut costs more.

What is the cost per square foot to remodel a house in the Bay Area?

In the Bay Area, remodeling runs about $100 to $500 per square foot. Cosmetic refreshes sit at $100 to $200 per sq ft, mid-range whole-house remodels at $200 to $400, and high-end or full-gut work at $400 to $500 and up. On a 2,000 sq ft home that spread produces a $200,000 to $1,000,000 range.

How much does a mid-range whole-house remodel cost?

A mid-range whole-house remodel runs $200 to $400 per square foot, so a 2,000 sq ft home lands around $400,000 to $800,000. That level covers a new kitchen and bathrooms, updated finishes throughout, and typical systems upgrades, without the custom materials and structural reconfiguration of a luxury or full-gut project.

What is the most expensive part of a remodel?

The kitchen is almost always the most expensive single part of a whole-house remodel, often 20 to 30 percent of the budget, because it combines cabinetry, countertops, appliances, plumbing, and electrical in one room. Bathrooms are the next most cost-intensive per square foot for the same reason.

How much should I budget for contingency?

Hold a contingency of 10 to 20 percent of the construction budget. On a $400,000 to $800,000 mid-range remodel that is roughly $40,000 to $160,000 set aside for surprises found once walls are opened - more toward the higher end for older pre-1980 Bay Area homes.
