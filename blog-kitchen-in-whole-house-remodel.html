The Kitchen in a Whole House Remodel

When you remodel a whole house, the kitchen is rarely just one room on the list - it is the room the rest of the plan bends around. It sets the open-concept layout, decides which wall comes down, and carries more plumbing, electrical, and cabinetry than any other space in the home. Get the kitchen right early and the whole project flows; treat it as an afterthought and it forces expensive rework in every connected system. This guide explains how the kitchen fits into a whole house remodel in the Bay Area - what it costs inside the larger budget, how it coordinates with layout and systems, and where it lands in the build sequence. It pairs with our home remodeling service and the broader whole house remodel cost guide.

Why the kitchen anchors a whole house remodel

The kitchen anchors a full remodel because almost every major decision in the project runs through it. In most Bay Area homes the kitchen is the hub of the ground floor, so the open-concept layout, the sightlines from the living area, and the flow to a dining room or patio are all defined by where the kitchen sits and how it opens up. Decide the kitchen and you have effectively decided the shape of the main floor.

It is also the most systems-heavy room in the house. A kitchen concentrates plumbing supply and drainage, a dense electrical load for appliances and lighting, gas or a high-amperage circuit for the range, and ventilation for the hood - all in one compact footprint. Because a whole house remodel opens walls across the home anyway, the kitchen is where that shared access pays off most: relocating a sink or adding circuits while the framing is exposed costs a fraction of what the same change costs later. That is why, in our process, the kitchen is designed first and the rest of the plan is coordinated around it, not the other way round. For a room-level view of the same work, see our San Jose kitchen remodel guide.

Kitchen cost within a whole house remodel (2026)

Inside a Bay Area whole house remodel, the kitchen typically takes 20 to 30 percent of the total budget - the single largest room-level share. In 2026 Santa Clara County numbers, plan on $45,000 to $85,000 for a mid-range kitchen and $140,000 to $225,000+ for a high-end one, on top of the rest of the project. The table below shows the tiers as they sit within a full remodel.

Kitchen tierCost in a full remodel (2026)What it includes
Cosmetic / light refresh$15,000-$35,000New countertops, appliances, paint, and hardware with cabinet refacing. Rare in a whole-home job, since the walls are already open.
Mid-range remodel$45,000-$85,000Semi-custom cabinets, quartz counters, tile backsplash, new appliances, updated plumbing and electrical on a standard 150-200 sq ft footprint.
High-end / luxury$140,000-$225,000+Custom millwork, natural stone, pro-grade appliances, wall removal, relocated plumbing and electrical, and a custom island.

Two things shift these numbers inside a whole-home project. First, the kitchen shares scaffolding, permits, dumpsters, and crew mobilization with the rest of the build, so some overhead that a standalone kitchen would carry alone is spread across the larger job. Second, layout changes that would be prohibitive on their own - moving the sink, opening a wall, rerouting a gas line - become affordable because the walls are already open. The result is that a mid-range kitchen inside a gut remodel often delivers more change per dollar than the same kitchen done in isolation. How the kitchen coordinates with the rest of the house

The kitchen touches more of the house than any other room, so in a full remodel it has to be coordinated across four fronts at once: open concept, walls, systems, and structure. Treating any of them as a separate decision is where budgets slip.

Open concept and layout. Most whole-home remodels aim to connect the kitchen to the living and dining areas. That decision is made at the kitchen, because the island position, the run of cabinets, and the appliance wall determine how the combined space reads and flows. The kitchen layout and the great-room layout are one drawing, not two.

Walls. The wall you remove for open concept is usually a kitchen wall, and whether it is load-bearing changes everything downstream. A load-bearing wall means a beam, posts, and often a revised foundation point, which affects ceiling height, lighting, and the schedule. Our guide to removing walls for an open floor plan covers the structural side in depth.

Systems. A whole house remodel is the moment to right-size plumbing, electrical, gas, and HVAC, and the kitchen is the heaviest draw on all of them. Coordinating the kitchen with a panel upgrade, new supply lines, and hood venting while the walls are open avoids re-opening finished surfaces later.

Structure. Islands with sinks or cooktops need plumbing and electrical brought through the floor, and a second story or ceiling change above the kitchen can add framing work. Locking the kitchen footprint before framing lets the structural and systems trades do their work once.

Key kitchen design decisions in a full remodel

A handful of kitchen decisions drive most of the cost and most of the daily experience of the finished home. Making them early, as part of the whole-house design, keeps the budget honest and the schedule intact.

  • Cabinetry tier. This is where a kitchen is won or lost. Moving from stock to semi-custom to custom cabinets accounts for most of the spread between a $45K and a $90K kitchen at the same footprint, so the cabinet decision comes first, not last.
  • Island and layout. Whether you add an island - and whether it carries a sink, a cooktop, or seating - sets the plumbing and electrical routing and the open-concept flow. It is a layout decision with structural consequences.
  • Countertops and backsplash. Material choice, from mid-grade quartz to natural stone, shifts both budget and maintenance. In a full remodel it should relate to the finishes used elsewhere in the home so the ground floor reads as one design.
  • Appliance tier and placement. Pro-grade appliances add load and venting requirements, and their locations fix where gas, water, and circuits must land. Choosing them during design, not after cabinets are ordered, prevents costly rework.

Because these choices ripple into structure and systems, they belong in the design phase alongside the rest of the house. That is the value of designing the whole home as one package rather than room by room - a point our home remodeling service is built around.

How to sequence the kitchen in the build

In a whole house remodel the kitchen is not a separate mini-project - it is woven into the master schedule so its trades line up with the same work happening across the home. The rough-in stages run house-wide, then the kitchen follows the standard finish order.

StageKitchen work in the whole-home sequence
DemolitionKitchen stripped alongside the rest of the house; walls opened for shared access.
Structural and framingLoad-bearing wall removed, beam and posts set, island framing and any ceiling changes done while other framing is underway.
Rough-in (plumbing, electrical, HVAC, gas)Sink, range, island, and appliance runs installed with the house-wide rough-in - the cheapest time to move anything.
Inspection, insulation, drywallKitchen walls closed with the rest of the home after rough-in passes inspection.
FinishesFlooring, then cabinets, countertops, backsplash, appliances, and final plumbing and electrical connections.

The reason to sequence this way is simple: access. When a wall is open once, every trade that needs it should do its work in that window. Pulling the kitchen out of the master schedule and treating it as a standalone job means opening the same walls twice and paying for the access twice. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the kitchen cost within a whole house remodel?

In a Bay Area whole house remodel, budget roughly $45,000 to $85,000 for a mid-range kitchen and $140,000 to $225,000+ for a high-end one, on top of the rest of the project. The kitchen typically absorbs 20 to 30 percent of a whole-home budget because it carries the most cabinetry, appliances, plumbing, and electrical work of any room.

Should the kitchen be planned first in a whole house remodel?

Design it first and design it with the whole plan, not as a standalone room. In a full remodel the kitchen usually drives the open-concept layout, the wall you remove, and where the new plumbing and electrical runs, so its footprint and appliance locations should be locked before framing starts. Deciding the kitchen late forces expensive rework in every connected system.

Can I move plumbing and change the kitchen layout during a whole house remodel?

Yes, and it is cheaper to do it now than later. When the walls are already open for a whole-home remodel, moving the sink, range, or island and adding circuits costs far less than opening finished walls for a standalone kitchen job. That shared access is one of the main financial advantages of remodeling the kitchen as part of the larger project.

What are the biggest kitchen design decisions in a full remodel?

Cabinetry drives most of the spread. Moving from stock to semi-custom to custom cabinets accounts for most of the difference between a $45K and a $90K kitchen at the same footprint. After cabinets, the big decisions are the island and layout, countertop material, appliance tier, and whether you remove a wall for open concept.

How is the kitchen sequenced in a whole house remodel?

The kitchen is sequenced with the whole house, not as a separate mini-project. Structural and rough-in work such as framing, plumbing, electrical, and HVAC happens while walls across the home are open, then the kitchen follows the standard order: drywall, flooring, cabinets, countertops, backsplash, appliances, and final connections. Coordinating it inside the master schedule avoids paying twice for access.

Key Takeaways

  • The kitchen anchors a whole house remodel - it sets the open-concept layout, drives the systems, and takes 20 to 30 percent of the budget.
  • In 2026 Bay Area pricing, plan on $45,000 to $85,000 for a mid-range kitchen and $140,000 to $225,000+ for high-end, inside the larger project.
  • Coordinate the kitchen across open concept, walls, systems, and structure at once, and lock its footprint before framing.
  • Sequence the kitchen inside the master schedule so you never open the same walls - or pay for access - twice.
