Gut Renovation Cost in the Bay Area

A full gut renovation (down to the studs) in the Bay Area typically costs $200 to $500+ per square foot, more than a cosmetic remodel because it replaces framing repairs, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, insulation, and every finish. For a 2,000 sq ft home that is roughly $400,000 to $1,000,000+. Bay Area labor rates and permit requirements push most gut projects toward the higher end of the range.

"Gut renovation" is one of the most misused phrases in remodeling, and the confusion costs homeowners money. A true gut strips a home down to the studs and rebuilds nearly everything behind the walls, which is why it lands at $200 to $500+ per square foot instead of the $100 to $200 a cosmetic refresh costs. This guide breaks down what a gut actually includes, what it costs in the Bay Area, why it costs more, and when the price is justified. It pairs with our whole house remodel cost guide and our renovation cost per square foot breakdown.

What is a gut renovation?

A gut renovation removes everything down to the structural framing - drywall, flooring, cabinets, wiring, plumbing, HVAC, and insulation all come out - leaving bare studs, subfloor, and often the foundation exposed. From there the home is rebuilt with new systems and finishes. It sits at the far end of a spectrum:

  • Cosmetic remodel ($100-200/sf). Structure and systems stay in place. You update surfaces: paint, flooring, cabinets, countertops, fixtures. Nothing behind the walls changes.
  • Partial gut ($200-350/sf). Specific areas are taken to the studs - typically the kitchen, bathrooms, and any wall being moved - while sound parts of the house are left intact. Common when only some systems have failed.
  • Full down-to-studs gut ($300-500+/sf). The entire home is stripped and rebuilt: full rewire, repipe, new HVAC, new insulation, new drywall, and all new finishes, often with structural and layout changes.

The dividing line is what happens behind the walls. A cosmetic remodel touches the visible layer; a gut replaces the hidden systems that make a home safe and efficient. That single difference is what moves the price into a different bracket, and it is why an accurate quote depends on how deep the demolition goes.

How much does a gut renovation cost?

A gut renovation in the Bay Area runs $200 to $500+ per square foot depending on how deep you go, with a full down-to-studs gut typically at $300 to $500+ per square foot. For a 2,000 sq ft home, a full gut lands around $600,000 to $1,000,000 or more. The table below shows how the three levels compare against the broader whole-house remodel range of $100 to $500 per square foot (mid-range $200 to $400).

LevelCost per sq ft2,000 sq ft example
Cosmetic remodel$100-200/sf$200,000 - $400,000
Partial gut$200-350/sf$400,000 - $700,000
Full down-to-studs gut$300-500+/sf$600,000 - $1,000,000+

Two Bay Area realities push most projects toward the higher end. First, labor is among the most expensive in the country, and a gut is labor-heavy from demolition to finish carpentry. Second, permits and inspections are required for the structural, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work a gut involves, which adds review time and fees. Why a gut costs more than a cosmetic remodel

A gut costs two to three times more per square foot than a cosmetic remodel because it replaces the hidden systems, not just the surfaces. Every item below is work a cosmetic remodel skips entirely:

Cost driverWhy it adds cost
Full rewireNew wiring throughout, plus a modern electrical panel and circuits sized for today's loads
RepipeAll supply and drain lines replaced, common where galvanized or cast iron has corroded
New HVACNew ducting, equipment, and often a properly sized system the old home never had
Structural & foundation repairsFraming, beams, and foundation issues surface once walls are open - frequent in pre-1980 homes
Insulation & drywallOpen walls get fully re-insulated and re-drywalled, a whole-home cost a refresh avoids
Code upgradesElectrical panel, egress windows, and current building codes must be met once systems are touched
Hazardous material abatementAsbestos and lead in older homes require licensed, tested removal before demolition continues

In older Bay Area housing stock these are not edge cases - they are the norm. A pre-1980 home usually needs at least a rewire, a repipe, and code upgrades, and abatement is common. That is why a gut budget must carry a real contingency: the walls almost always reveal something. A cosmetic remodel avoids all of this by leaving the systems alone, which is exactly why it is cheaper and also why it is not an option when those systems have reached end of life.

When a gut renovation is worth it

A gut renovation is worth it when the cost of a full rebuild of systems is justified by the home's condition or your plans. It usually makes sense in four situations:

  • Failing systems. When wiring, plumbing, or HVAC are at end of life, patching around them wastes money. A gut replaces everything once, so you are not tearing out new finishes in five years to fix what was left behind.
  • Pre-1980 homes. Older Bay Area homes often combine outdated wiring, corroded plumbing, no insulation, and hazardous materials. A gut is frequently the only way to bring the whole home up to current safety and efficiency standards.
  • Major layout change. Moving walls, opening up floor plans, or relocating kitchens and baths means opening the structure anyway - at which point a full gut is often only marginally more than a partial one.
  • Buying a fixer. A home bought below market because of its condition can pencil out when the purchase price plus a gut is still below a comparable move-in-ready home. Our fixer-upper renovation guide walks through that math.

Compare the three paths before committing. A cosmetic or partial remodel ($100-350/sf) is the right call when the structure and most systems are sound. A full gut ($300-500+/sf) fits when the systems are failing but the shell and location are worth keeping. A rebuild can cost about the same as a full gut once foundation and framing are failing across the whole home, so when a gut approaches the price of new construction, weigh starting over - our remodel vs rebuild guide covers that fork in detail.

How to control gut renovation cost

You cannot make a gut cheap, but you can keep it from running away. The biggest savings come from decisions made before demolition:

  • Design first, price second. A complete design and set of plans lets a builder quote the real scope instead of guessing. Vague scope is what produces the change orders that blow past budget. This is the core of our design package.
  • Keep the footprint and plumbing walls where you can. Moving the building envelope or relocating kitchens and baths adds structural, plumbing, and permitting cost fast. Reworking within the existing footprint and keeping wet walls in place is one of the largest levers on the final number.
  • Hold a 10-20% contingency. A gut opens every wall, and older homes reveal surprises. A reserve keeps the project moving instead of stalling when the framing or plumbing needs more than expected.
  • Use one design-build team. When the same team designs, prices, permits, and builds, the budget is accurate from the start and there are no handoffs where scope and cost drift apart. That is how our home remodeling and architecture and permitting work fit together.

Permitting is part of the cost and the timeline, not an afterthought - a gut needs permits for structural, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work, filed in San Jose at SJPermits.org. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a gut renovation cost?

A full gut renovation down to the studs in the Bay Area typically costs $200 to $500+ per square foot. That is higher than a cosmetic remodel because it replaces framing repairs, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, insulation, and every finish. For a 2,000 sq ft home that is roughly $400,000 to $1,000,000 or more, with Bay Area labor and permits pushing most projects toward the higher end.

What is the difference between a gut and a cosmetic remodel?

A cosmetic remodel keeps the structure and systems in place and updates surfaces such as paint, flooring, cabinets, and fixtures, usually $100 to $200 per square foot. A gut renovation strips the home to the studs and replaces wiring, plumbing, HVAC, insulation, and finishes, running $200 to $500+ per square foot because far more work happens behind the walls.

How much does it cost to gut a 2,000 sq ft house?

A full down-to-studs gut of a 2,000 sq ft house in the Bay Area typically runs $600,000 to $1,000,000 or more, based on $300 to $500+ per square foot. A partial gut of the same home lands closer to $400,000 to $700,000. The final number depends on the age of the home, foundation and structural work, and how high the finish level is.

Is a gut renovation worth it?

A gut renovation is usually worth it when the home has failing systems, is a pre-1980 house with outdated wiring and plumbing, needs a major layout change, or is a fixer bought below market. If the structure is sound and only surfaces are dated, a cosmetic or partial remodel is cheaper. If the foundation and framing are failing across the whole home, a rebuild can cost about the same, so compare all three before committing.

Does a gut renovation need permits?

Yes. A gut renovation almost always needs permits because it touches structural, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work, all of which are inspected. In San Jose these are filed at SJPermits.org. Work done without permits can be red-tagged and redone, so permitting is built into the timeline and budget from the start.

Key Takeaways

  • A full gut renovation runs $200 to $500+ per square foot in the Bay Area, versus $100 to $200 for a cosmetic remodel.
  • Gutting a 2,000 sq ft home costs roughly $400,000 to $1,000,000+, with a full down-to-studs gut at $600,000 to $1,000,000+.
  • The extra cost is the hidden work: full rewire, repipe, new HVAC, structural repairs, insulation, code upgrades, and abatement.
  • Design first, keep the footprint and plumbing walls, hold a 10-20% contingency, and use one design-build team for accurate pricing.
