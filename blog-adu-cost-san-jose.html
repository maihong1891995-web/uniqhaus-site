If you are pricing an accessory dwelling unit in Santa Clara County, the short answer is this: most San Jose ADUs cost about $150,000 to $400,000 all-in. A junior ADU or a simple garage conversion can come in below that range, and a large detached unit on a difficult site can run above it. Where you fall in that range depends almost entirely on the type of ADU, its size, and how much new utility infrastructure the site needs. Below we break the numbers down by ADU type, show what San Jose actually charges in permit and impact fees, and cover how homeowners finance the build. The dollar figures here are 2026 market ranges, not fixed quotes - every lot is different, and this guide is meant to help you plan before you talk to a design-build team.

How much does an ADU cost in San Jose in 2026?

Most San Jose ADUs cost $150,000 to $400,000 all-in, and the type of unit does most of the work in deciding where you land. A junior ADU carved out of an existing bedroom is the cheapest path; a large, freestanding detached ADU with its own utilities is the most expensive. What does each ADU type cost - garage conversion vs. attached vs. detached vs. JADU?

The cheapest ADU to build in San Jose is a JADU or a garage conversion, and the most expensive is a large detached unit - the gap comes down to how much structure and utility work already exists. A JADU ($30K-$100K) reuses space inside your home, so there is no new foundation, roof, or exterior shell. A garage conversion ($120K-$150K) already has a slab and walls, which is why it prices at $250-$380/sq ft instead of $350-$500. Our garage conversion service is often the fastest route to a legal second unit for homeowners who already have a detached or attached garage.

An attached ADU (~$300/sq ft) shares walls and utility infrastructure with the main house, which trims cost versus building freestanding. A detached ADU ($250K-$400K) is a standalone building - new foundation, framing, roof, and independent water, sewer, and electrical service - which is why it sits at the top of the range. Utility connections alone add $5,000-$8,000 for a shared (attached or garage) hookup and $10,000-$15,000+ for the independent connections a detached unit requires. New sewer and water lines can add another $5,000-$12,000 depending on how far your lines have to run.

Is a guest house cheaper to build than an ADU in San Jose?

Usually yes, because a guest house is a smaller, simpler building, but in San Jose it cannot do what an ADU does. A full ADU in the Bay Area typically costs $150,000 to $400,000 all-in. A kitchen-free guest house skips several of the most expensive line items, so it often lands below that, depending on size, foundation, and finishes.

Here is where the cost gap comes from:

  • No kitchen. Cabinets, counters, appliances, venting, and the dedicated electrical circuits they need are some of the largest costs in a small unit.
  • Limited plumbing. San Jose's accessory building handout caps a backyard accessory building at two plumbing fixtures, such as a toilet and a sink. That means no full bath and far less drain and water line work.
  • Simpler utilities. A guest house is usually fed from the main house, so it rarely needs the independent water, sewer, and electrical service that a new detached ADU may require.
  • The same structural basics. Foundation, framing, roof, windows, insulation, and a building permit still apply, so a well-built guest house is not a shed budget.

The trade-off is how you can use it. San Jose does not allow living space or sleeping quarters in an accessory building, so a guest house cannot be rented as a home or used as a bedroom for a parent. If anyone will sleep there or pay rent, the legal path is an ADU, and the extra cost buys a second legal dwelling that can produce income. Adding a kitchen and shower to a guest house later turns it into an ADU, which means a new ADU permit plus plumbing, energy, and fire-safety upgrades. Redoing work is almost always more expensive than designing for the final use from the start.

A practical rule: decide who will use the space first, then price the guest house and the ADU side by side before anyone draws a wall. If an ADU is likely within a few years, plan the slab for future drain lines and size the electrical service with room to grow. Our guest house vs ADU guide covers the legal differences in detail. What drives ADU cost in San Jose?

Five factors drive most of the price difference between a $150K and a $400K ADU: size, foundation, utilities, finishes, and site conditions. Understanding them helps you steer the budget before design even starts.

  • Size and type. Square footage is the biggest lever, and reusing existing structure (JADU, garage conversion) removes the most expensive line items.
  • Foundation and structure. A new detached slab and framing package is a major cost that garage conversions largely skip.
  • Utilities. Independent water, sewer, and electrical for a detached unit runs $10K-$15K+; sharing existing service keeps it to $5K-$8K.
  • Finishes. Cabinetry, countertops, flooring, and fixtures can swing the per-square-foot number by $100 or more.
  • Site conditions. Sloped lots, poor soils, long utility runs, tree protection, and tight access all add cost that is invisible until a site is properly assessed.

What are San Jose ADU permit and impact fees in 2026?

San Jose permit fees for a small ADU run about $3,500-$6,000 for units of 750 sq ft or less, and city impact fees are waived for those units under state law. That waiver is one of the strongest financial reasons to keep an ADU at or under 750 square feet of interior livable space.

Fee typeADU up to 750 sq ftADU 750-1,200 sq ft
Permit fees (plan check + issuance)$3,500 - $6,000$6,000 - $14,000
Impact fees (parkland, traffic, other city fees)Waived (state law)Proportional to the ADU-to-home size ratio
School district feesWaived at 500 sq ft or less; may apply above 500 sq ftMay apply; confirm with your school district
Utility connection fees$5,000 - $12,000 if new connections required$5,000 - $12,000 if new connections required

State law bars impact fees on an ADU with 750 sq ft or less of interior livable space, and on a JADU of 500 sq ft or less. The rule began with SB 13 (2019) and now sits in Government Code section 66324, after California moved its ADU laws out of the old section 65852.2 in 2024. SB 543, in effect since January 1, 2026, clarified that size is measured as interior livable space. Larger ADUs pay impact fees in proportion to the ADU's size compared with the main home. School district fees follow their own rule: they are waived for units of 500 sq ft or less, but a district may still charge them on a larger ADU, so confirm with your district. Water and sewer connection or capacity charges are not impact fees and can still apply. City fee schedules update regularly, so treat the permit figures as 2026 planning ranges and check current numbers with the San Jose Building Fee Estimator. How long does an ADU permit take in San Jose?

California law requires San Jose to complete plan review within 60 days or the ADU is automatically approved - but real-world timelines run 2 to 8 months depending on how complete your plans are. The 60-day clock (set by SB 13 in 2019, with the deemed-approved rule added by AB 2221 in 2022) only starts once the city has a complete application. Since January 1, 2026, SB 543 also requires the city to tell you within 15 business days whether your application is complete. Plan quality is what actually controls your schedule.

San Jose runs a preapproved ADU program - standard plans already vetted by the city - which is the fastest and cheapest path, with same-day permit issuance possible when your site plan is complete and accurate. Statewide, AB 1332 (2023) required every California city and county to set up a preapproved ADU plan program by January 1, 2025, and San Jose already had one in place. The single biggest thing you can do to hit the 60-day law is submit complete, accurate drawings the first time.

What do the 2026 California ADU laws mean for me?

Three state laws - AB 68, AB 976, and AB 1033 - have made San Jose one of the most ADU-friendly cities in California in 2026. Together they removed old barriers on lot size, owner-occupancy, and even whether you can sell the unit separately.

  • AB 68 (2019): removed minimum lot size requirements and cut side and rear setbacks to just 4 feet.
  • AB 976 (2023): permanently bans owner-occupancy requirements, so you do not have to live on the property to build and rent an ADU. (The separate impact-fee waiver for ADUs under 750 sq ft comes from SB 13, 2019.)
  • AB 1033 (2023): lets ADUs be sold separately like a condo where the city opts in - and San Jose has opted in, adopting its ordinance in 2024 as the first city in California to do so. A separate sale still requires a condo map and a homeowners association.
  • 2026 updates (SB 543 and AB 1154): ADU and JADU size limits and the fee thresholds are now measured as interior livable space, cities must confirm completeness within 15 business days, and a JADU owner only has to live on site if the JADU shares a bathroom with the main house. The HCD ADU Handbook summarizes these changes.

On size, state law lets cities allow detached ADUs up to 1,200 sq ft, and a city cannot cap them below 850 sq ft (one bedroom or less) or 1,000 sq ft (two or more bedrooms). In San Jose, the local limit for a detached ADU depends on lot size; city materials list 1,000 sq ft on lots under 9,000 sq ft and 1,200 sq ft on larger lots. Separately, state law guarantees room for an 800 sq ft ADU with 4-foot side and rear setbacks even where lot coverage or floor-area limits would otherwise block it, and every ADU application is reviewed ministerially, with no public hearing. Setbacks are 4 ft on the side and rear for a detached unit, with the front following your zone's standard. And you're allowed one ADU plus one JADU on a single-family lot. How much rent can an ADU earn in San Jose?

A 600-800 sq ft ADU in San Jose rents for $1,800-$2,800 per month in 2026, and a well-finished detached unit can push well above that. For a detached ADU renting at $2,500-$4,500 a month, that is roughly $30,000-$54,000 per year in gross rent, before taxes, insurance, maintenance, and vacancy.

How do you finance an ADU in San Jose?

Most San Jose homeowners finance an ADU through home equity - a HELOC or cash-out refinance - because Bay Area property values have built substantial equity to draw on. There are four common paths, and the right one depends on your equity position, rate environment, and cash flow. Confirm specifics with a lender, as terms and rates change.

  • HELOC / home equity loan: borrow against existing equity; flexible draws that suit a phased build.
  • Cash-out refinance: replace your mortgage with a larger one and take the difference as cash - attractive when you can hold a competitive rate.
  • Renovation loans: Fannie Mae HomeStyle and Freddie Mac CHOICERenovation let you finance construction based on the home's projected after-completion value.
  • ADU-specific and construction loans: purpose-built products that underwrite the future rental income, plus construction loans that convert to a permanent mortgage.

Because financing hinges on accurate scope and cost, a clear design-build estimate up front makes lender conversations far smoother. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to build an ADU in San Jose in 2026?

Most San Jose ADUs cost about $150,000 to $400,000 all-in. A JADU or simple garage conversion can cost less, and most detached ADUs land between $250,000 and $400,000, or about $350-$500 per square foot.

Are impact fees waived for small ADUs in San Jose?

Yes. Under state law (SB 13, 2019), city impact fees are waived for ADUs with 750 square feet or less of interior livable space. School district fees are waived only at 500 square feet or less, so a larger ADU may still owe them. Permit fees for those units still run about $3,500-$6,000.

Can I sell my ADU separately in San Jose?

Yes. Under AB 1033, cities can allow ADUs to be sold separately like a condo, and San Jose has opted in. That makes a separately sellable ADU a real resale advantage in San Jose.

Do I need to live on the property if I build an ADU?

No. AB 976 permanently banned owner-occupancy requirements for ADUs in California, so you can build and rent an ADU without living on the property yourself.

How long does it take to get an ADU permit in San Jose?

State law requires San Jose to complete plan review within 60 days or the ADU is auto-approved. Real-world timelines run 2 to 8 months depending on plan completeness; using a city preapproved plan can allow same-day permit issuance.

How much rent can an ADU earn in San Jose?

A 600-800 sq ft ADU rents for $1,800-$2,800 per month in 2026, while a $300K-plus detached unit can earn $2,500-$4,500. At those rents, a detached unit brings in roughly $30,000-$54,000 a year in gross rent, before expenses.

How much does an in-law suite ADU cost in San Jose?

An in-law suite built as an ADU in San Jose typically costs $150,000 to $400,000 all-in, depending on size, whether it is attached or detached, and how much new utility work the site needs. A JADU carved out of space inside your home is the lower-cost route, but if it shares a bathroom with the main house, the owner must live on the property.
