How Long Does a Whole House Remodel Take?

A whole house remodel takes about 6 to 12 months in the Bay Area - roughly 2 to 3 months of design and permitting plus 4 to 9 months of construction. A cosmetic-only refresh is faster at 2 to 4 months, while a full down-to-studs gut runs 9 to 12 months or more. Permitting with the City of San Jose is the single biggest variable in the schedule.

Timeline is usually the first question a homeowner asks after cost, and the honest answer is a range, because a whole house remodel is really two projects back to back: the paperwork phase and the build phase. Get the first one moving early and the whole schedule tightens. This guide breaks down how long a whole house remodel takes in the Bay Area, phase by phase and by scope, so you can plan around a realistic calendar instead of a hopeful one. It pairs with our whole house remodel cost guide and the where to start guide.

How long does a whole house remodel take from start to finish?

A whole house remodel takes about 6 to 12 months from your first design meeting to move-back-in day. That total splits into two stages: roughly 2 to 3 months of design and permitting, then 4 to 9 months of construction. The design-and-permit stage is where projects quietly lose time, because a permit application sitting in plan check does not feel like progress even though it is on the critical path.

The construction stage is more predictable once permits are issued, but its length scales directly with scope. Swapping finishes and reconfiguring a kitchen is a different animal from moving load-bearing walls and re-leveling a foundation. That is why the same phrase - "whole house remodel" - can mean a 6-month project for one family and a 14-month project for their neighbor. The way to compress the total is not to rush the build; it is to run design and architecture and permitting in parallel so the paperwork stage overlaps rather than stacks.

Timeline phase by phase

Broken into its eight working phases, a typical whole house remodel spends about 8 to 20 weeks on design and permitting and the balance on construction. The table below shows realistic durations for a mid-range Bay Area project; phases can overlap slightly, but most gate the next one.

PhaseTypical durationNotes
Design & planning4-8 weeksConcept, drawings, selections, and pricing before permits
Permitting / plan check with City of San Jose4-12 weeksThe biggest schedule variable; correction cycles add time
Demolition1-2 weeksStrip finishes and expose structure and systems
Structure / framing3-6 weeksFoundation work, load-bearing changes, and additions
Rough systems (electrical, plumbing, HVAC)3-5 weeksRun in open walls, then rough inspections
Insulation & drywall2-4 weeksClose the walls after rough inspections pass
Finishes6-10 weeksFlooring, cabinets, tile, paint, fixtures - the longest build phase
Final inspection & punch list1-2 weeksSign-off, touch-ups, and closeout before move-in

Finishes are the longest single construction phase because they involve the most trades and the most selections - cabinets, countertops, tile, flooring, and fixtures each carry their own lead time. How scope changes the timeline

Scope moves the total by 6 months or more between the lightest and heaviest projects. A cosmetic refresh can wrap in a single season, while a structural gut can run past a year. The table below maps the three common scopes to construction time and realistic totals including design and permits.

ScopeConstruction timeTotal with design + permits
Cosmetic refresh6-10 weeks3-4 months
Mid-range whole-house ($200-400/sf)4-6 months6-9 months
Full down-to-studs gut / structural7-10 months9-12+ months

Cost tracks scope the same way it tracks time. A whole house remodel generally runs $100 to $500 per square foot, with most mid-range Bay Area projects landing between $200 and $400 per square foot. If you are still setting a budget, the cost guide for San Jose breaks those ranges down by level and home size. A cosmetic refresh stays short because it skips permitting-heavy structural work; a gut takes longest because nearly every phase in the table above is on the critical path.

What makes a remodel take longer

Most schedule slips trace back to five predictable causes, and four of them are avoidable with planning. Knowing them up front is how you build a calendar that holds:

  • Permitting delays. Plan check with the City of San Jose can stretch when a submittal draws corrections and has to be revised and resubmitted. Designing to code from the start and submitting a complete package is the fix.
  • Structural surprises in older homes. Pre-1980 Bay Area homes often hide knob-and-tube wiring, corroded plumbing, or foundation issues that only appear once walls open. A thorough inspection during design shrinks the surprises.
  • Material and product lead times. Cabinets, windows, tile, and specialty fixtures can carry lead times of many weeks; ordering late stalls the finishes phase even when the crew is ready.
  • Change orders. Every mid-build decision to move a wall or swap a finish ripples through the schedule. Finalizing the design before demolition keeps changes off the critical path.
  • Phasing while living in the home. Remodeling around an occupied household is slower than an empty jobsite, because work areas have to be sealed, cleaned, and rotated.

The one cause you cannot fully control is permitting, which is exactly why we treat it as the schedule's anchor and start submittal as early as the drawings allow.

How to keep the timeline on track

The four moves below can save weeks to months on a 6 to 12 month remodel, and every one of them happens before or at the start of construction:

  • Run design and permit submittal in parallel. Do not wait for a fully finished design to start the permit conversation. Overlapping these two stages is the single biggest lever on the total timeline.
  • Finalize every selection before construction. Lock cabinets, tile, flooring, fixtures, and finishes before demolition so long-lead items are ordered and nothing stalls the finishes phase.
  • Hold a contingency. A 10 to 20 percent reserve keeps the project moving when walls open and reveal surprises, instead of pausing while you find more budget.
  • Use one design-build team. A single team from design through build removes the handoffs between architect, designer, and contractor where schedules usually break down.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a whole house remodel take?

A whole house remodel takes about 6 to 12 months in the Bay Area, made up of roughly 2 to 3 months of design and permitting plus 4 to 9 months of construction. A cosmetic refresh can finish in 2 to 4 months, while a full down-to-studs gut or a project with structural changes runs 9 to 12 months or more.

How long does the design and permitting phase take?

Design and permitting typically take about 2 to 3 months before construction begins - roughly 4 to 8 weeks for design and drawings and another 4 to 12 weeks for plan check with the City of San Jose. Running design and permit submittal in parallel is the most reliable way to keep this phase short.

How long does a full gut renovation take?

A full down-to-studs gut renovation takes about 9 to 12 months or more in total, with roughly 7 to 10 months of construction on top of design and permitting. Structural changes, older pre-1980 homes, and long material lead times are what push a gut toward the top of that range.

Can you speed up a whole house remodel?

Yes, but the fastest lever is preparation, not rushing the build. Running design and permit submittal in parallel, finalizing every selection before construction starts, holding a 10 to 20 percent contingency, and using one design-build team with no handoffs can save weeks or months on a 6 to 12 month project.

Do permits in San Jose slow down a remodel?

Permitting is usually the single biggest schedule variable on a Bay Area remodel. Plan check with the City of San Jose through SJPermits.org can take about 4 to 12 weeks depending on scope and how many correction cycles a submittal goes through, which is why we submit early and design to code from the start.

Key Takeaways

  • A whole house remodel takes about 6 to 12 months total - roughly 2 to 3 months of design and permitting plus 4 to 9 months of construction.
  • Scope sets the range: a cosmetic refresh runs 3 to 4 months, a mid-range whole-house 6 to 9 months, and a full gut 9 to 12 months or more.
  • Permitting with the City of San Jose is the biggest variable, taking about 4 to 12 weeks depending on correction cycles.
  • Running design and permits in parallel, locking selections early, and holding a 10 to 20 percent contingency are the moves that keep the timeline on track.
